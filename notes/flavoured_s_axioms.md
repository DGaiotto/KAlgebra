# The flavoured `S` axiomatics — `\cE_G` and its BPS quiver constraint

**Origin.** The user, 2026-09-13, on reading the current formulation of the `S`
axioms: *"the new formulation of the S axioms allows for a principled rethink of
non-Abelian-flavoured BPS KAlgebras"*, and then the construction itself —

> Consider a flavour group `G`.  We can define a pronilpotent group `\cE_G` as
> products of `E^{(s;R)}_\fq(X_\gamma)` factors, each being a product of the form
> `\prod_{w\in R} E^{(s)}_\fq(\mu^w X_\gamma)`, with `\gamma \in \Gamma` now not
> including the flavour weight lattice.  And then `S` would be defined by the
> usual axiom, but the node charge monomials `X_{\gamma_a}` are now replaced by
> sequences `\mu^w X_{\gamma_a}` with `w` in representations `R_a` of `G`.
> Though in practice the group will be a product of `SU(N_a)` and `R_a` will be
> the `N_a`-dimensional irrep.

and the motivation:

> as soon as you have `N` identical nodes in the quiver, the theory has manifest
> `SU(N)` symmetry.  You can ignore it, but why?  So if you have groups of `N_a`
> nodes, you have `SU(N_a)` symmetry naturally.  On the other hand the reps in
> `S` and the spec will be more general.

**Purpose of this document.** To state the flavoured axioms precisely, in the
paper's notation, and to say for each one what has been *decided by computation*
and what has not — so that the axiomatics can go into the paper with its
evidence attached.  The evidence lives in
`tests/test_flavoured_factor_spectrum.py`, which is written so that each test
name **is** an axiom statement; the implementation is
`flavoured_factor_spectrum.py`.

**Notation.** The paper's: `\cG` for the ambient pro-nilpotent group, `\cE` for
the subgroup generated by the multiplets, `E^{(s)}_\fq(x)` for the multiplet
itself.  The repo's older spellings for these (`𝒢` for `\cE`, `G` for `\cG`,
`m_s(γ)` for the multiplet) were repo coinages and two of them were *crossed*
with the paper's; they were retired on 2026-09-13 by user ruling — *"No idea
where `m_s` came from, in code is OK but not in documents"* — and
`pronilpotent_group_conjecture.md` now carries the paper's symbols with a
notation box recording the change.  Reps are written `r_a` (user: *"use whatev
notations for the `R_a`, `r_a` may be OK"*), which also keeps `R` free for its
repo-wide meaning, the `Z₊`-ring `coefficient_ring()`.

## 1. The unflavoured statements being generalised

From the paper.  `\cG` is the pro-nilpotent group of elements
`1 + Σ_{γ∈Γ_+} g_γ(\fq) X_γ` with `g_γ ∈ Z[\fq, \fq^{-1}, (1-\fq^{2n})^{-1}]`;
`E_\fq(x) = (-\fq x; \fq^2)_∞^{-1}`; and for `s ∈ ½N`

    E^{(s)}_\fq(x)  =  ∏_{j=-s}^{s} E_\fq((-1)^{2s} \fq^{2j} x)^{(-1)^{2s}} .

`\cE ⊂ \cG` is generated by the `E^{(s)}_\fq(X_γ)^{±1}`, `γ ∈ Γ_+`.  A BPS
quiver RG flow satisfies the **BPS quiver constraint**

    S  =  1  -  \fq Σ_i X_{γ_i}  +  O(\fq^2) ,

and the two conjectures are **Uniqueness of `S`** (a unique `S ∈ \cE` satisfies
the constraint) and **PBW factorization** (every `g ∈ \cE` factors as
`∏_{γ,s} E^{(s)}_\fq(X_γ)^{Ω(γ,s)}`, in any total order on `Γ_+ × ½N`, with
integral exponents).

## 2. The flavoured definitions

Let `G` be a compact flavour group; in practice `G = ∏_a SU(N_a)`.  Let `μ^w`
denote the character of its maximal torus at the weight `w`; the `μ` are
**central** and carry `\fq`-degree zero.  For an irrep `r` of `G`, with the
product over the weights of `r` **taken with multiplicity**,

    E^{(s;r)}_\fq(X_γ)  :=  ∏_{w ∈ r} E^{(s)}_\fq(μ^w X_γ) ,

and `\cE_G` is the subgroup generated by the `E^{(s;r)}_\fq(X_γ)^{±1}` for
`γ ∈ Γ_+`, `s ∈ ½N` and `r ∈ Irr(G)`.  Here `Γ` is the **gauge** charge lattice
and does not contain the flavour weight lattice.

**Flavoured BPS quiver constraint.**  With node `a` carrying the irrep `r_a`,

    S  =  1  -  \fq Σ_a χ_{r_a}(μ) X_{γ_a}  +  O(\fq^2) .

**Flavoured PBW factorization.**  Every `g ∈ \cE_G` factors as
`∏_{γ,s,r} E^{(s;r)}_\fq(X_γ)^{Ω(γ,s,r)}`, the product taken in any total order
on `Γ_+ × ½N × Irr(G)`, with integral exponents — so the multiplicity `Ω(γ)` is
valued in `R(G) ⊗ P` rather than `P` alone (`P` = palindromic `Z[\fq^{±1}]`,
`Z`-basis the `χ_s`).  That is `R(G) ⊗ R(SU(2)_spin)`: a flavour-rep times
spin-rep multiplicity, which is what a BPS multiplicity is.

**No half-integrality arises** (user, 2026-09-13).  The half-integers familiar
from presentations that keep a flavour direction *inside* `Γ` and average the
node charges — the `(a, b, s)` labels of the `D_n`-chamber torus that was
`implementations/a1dn_kalg.py` until 2026-09-23 (now `legacy/a1dn_kalg_chamber_torus.py`), with
`b = (γ_{n-2}+γ_{n-1})/2 ∈ ½Z` — are an artifact of that frame.  Here `Γ` is the
gauge lattice alone and the weights are carried separately, in the integral
coordinates `(w_1-w_N, …, w_{N-1}-w_N)`; no midpoint is ever formed.

## 3. The one substantive claim, and why the rest is inherited

`μ^w X_γ` is `X_{γ+w}` in the lattice enlarged by the flavour weights, and the
enlarged charges `γ + w`, `w ∈ r`, pair to zero with one another — the flavour
directions are central and the gauge charge is shared.  Two consequences:

* every generator of `\cE_G` is an ordinary product of commuting `E^{(s)}`
  factors in the enlarged lattice, so **`\cE_G ⊆ \cE_enlarged`**;
* `χ_{r_a}(μ) X_{γ_a} = Σ_{w∈r_a} X_{γ_a+w}`, so the **flavoured constraint is
  literally the enlarged BPS quiver constraint**.

Hence flavoured uniqueness and flavoured order-independence are *inherited* from
the unflavoured conjectures applied to the enlarged quiver.  What is **not**
inherited, and is the whole content of the flavoured axiomatics, is

> **`S_enlarged ∈ \cE_G`** — the enlarged `S`'s PBW multiplicities, grouped by
> gauge charge, assemble into honest `G`-characters.

This is what the implementation is built to decide, and it is decided
*emergently*: the recursion forces `Ω` at each flavour weight **independently**
(legitimate because the `μ` are central and of `\fq`-degree zero, so the
`\fq`-triangularity that forces `Ω` unflavoured runs unchanged weight by
weight), and nothing anywhere imposes Weyl symmetry.  `Ω`'s Weyl invariance —
equivalently, its being a virtual character — is therefore an *output*.

## 4. The index set of the flavoured PBW statement is `Irr(G)`, not the weights

**This is the finding that most needs to be in the paper, because it is a real
constraint and it is invisible unflavoured.**

The flavoured PBW statement quantifies over total orders on
`Γ_+ × ½N × Irr(G)`.  It must: a whole `E^{(s;r)}_\fq(X_γ)` has to be placed as
one unit.  Ordering the individual weight factors `E^{(s)}_\fq(μ^w X_γ)`
independently is a perfectly good order in the *enlarged* `\cE`, and it leaves
`S` unchanged — but the resulting multiplicities are then **not** `G`-characters,
so it is not a factorization in `\cE_G` at all.

Measured (`test_a_weight_level_order_leaves_cE_G`), over random weight-level
orders on four quivers, 8 orders each:

| | result |
|---|---|
| `S` unchanged under a weight-level order | **32 / 32** |
| `Ω` fails to be a `G`-character | **4–5 of every 8** |

(The orders that do *not* break it are the ones that happen to keep each rep's
weights together.)  So the generator set of `\cE_G` is doing real work, and
"place whole reps" is not a convention but the definition of the group.

Implementation `flavoured_factor_spectrum.py`; battery
`tests/test_flavoured_factor_spectrum.py` (25 tests, all passing).  The
recursion runs on the **reduced** cone carrying a weight grading; the
independent ground truth runs the **unflavoured** engine
(`bps_factor_spectrum.BPSFactorSpectrum`) on the unfolded quiver.  The two share
the univariate ray arithmetic and nothing else.

## 6. The reps that occur are more general than the node reps

The user's *"the reps in `S` and the spec will be more general"*, made concrete.
With the `SU(2)` fundamental at a node of the `A2` quiver, the multiplicities
reach the adjoint and beyond — at `D = 6`, up to the 6-dimensional irrep; the
charges `γ = (n,1)` carry the `n`-dimensional irrep with alternating sign.  With
the `SU(3)` fundamental at a node, `Ω` reaches `(2,1)` — **the adjoint, whose
zero weight has multiplicity two**.

That last point is where the `R(G)`-valued bookkeeping stops being decorative
and becomes necessary.  A rep with a repeated weight cannot sit at a node of an
unfolded quiver, since two nodes would carry the same charge; the user's ruling
is that this never arises for *node* data, because *"the node irreps are
fundamentals of `SU(N_a)` in reality"* — and that ruling is what gives every
test its ground truth.  But it does arise for the **derived** multiplicities,
which is exactly where it cannot be worked around by unfolding.

`Ω` also mixes the two gradings genuinely: on the `A3` quiver with `SU(2)` at
the middle node, `Ω((1,2,1)) = χ_{1/2}·(1 ⊕ adjoint)` — a spin-½ multiplet
carrying a non-trivial flavour rep, i.e. an element of `R(G) ⊗ P` with neither
factor trivial.


## 6b. The flavoured spec of an acyclic quiver is its node data

On an **acyclic** flavoured quiver, the source/sink strip order gives

    S  =  ∏_a E^{(0;r_a)}_\fq(X_{γ_a}) ,

exactly `rank` factors — one per **reduced** node, at spin `0`, carrying
precisely that node's own rep and nothing else.  Measured on all nine acyclic
corpus entries (`SU(2)`, `SU(3)`, `SU(2)×SU(2)`, `SU(2)×SU(3)`; `A2`,
Kronecker-2 and -3, `A3` with the flavoured node at the middle and at an end,
`A1×A1`).  Where the strip leaves a core — the 3-cycle — it determines nothing
and says so, rather than being silently extended.

This is the flavoured form of the repo's mantle statement (acyclic ⇒
topological product spec), and it is a spec in the working sense: finite,
spin-`0`, positive.  It attains the floor, by the same argument as unflavoured:
at cone degree `1` the accumulated product is still empty, so every node carries
a factor in **every** order, and `rank` is the minimum.

**And this is where the construction is genuinely more economical.**  Both
routes attain their own floor on an acyclic quiver, so the factor counts are

    flavoured:  g = the number of reduced nodes
    unfolded:   Σ_a dim r_a = the number of unfolded nodes

— measured `2 vs 3` (`A2`, `SU(2)`), `2 vs 4` (`A2`, `SU(3)`), `2 vs 5` (`A2`,
`SU(2)×SU(3)`), `3 vs 4` (`A3`).  `\cE_G` packs each group of `N_a` unfolded
factors into one generator.  Note carefully that this is economy in the
**factorization**, not in the cone — the cone claim is retracted in §7.

## 7. What this does NOT buy, stated because it is easy to over-read

**It is not a cost win by itself.**  The reduced cone is smaller than the
enlarged one, but the flavour weights return as fibres over each reduced cone
point and the total accumulator size is unchanged.  A `|W|`-fold saving is
available by solving one `Ω` per Weyl orbit; it is deliberately **not** taken in
the implementation, because taking it would impose the Weyl invariance that is
currently the emergent claim.  (An earlier session assessment claimed the cone
shrink was a compounding cost win; that was wrong and is retracted here.)

What it buys instead: `Ω` becomes a `G`-character, so the reps are the natural
labels; the derived reps are expressible at all; the **factorization is shorter**
(§6b, `g` factors against `Σ_a dim r_a`); and the dictionary key becomes the
quiver-with-`G`-data rather than the unfolded quiver.


## 9. Towards a dictionary of quivers with `G` symmetry

User, 2026-09-13: *"ultimately we may culminate into a dictionary of quivers
with `G` symmetry, which will be a source of relative q-Langlands knowledge"*.
The enabling step is built — and since 2026-09-14/15 **so is the dictionary**:
`dictionaries/flavoured/`, the `SU(2)` set at total arrow weight `≤ 9`
(139,371 quivers, 723 KB gzipped, tracked), built by `dictionaries/build_flavoured.py`
and read through `dictionary_loader.lookup_flavoured`.  §9c below records what
it contains and how it is checked.

**The entry identity.**  The flavour datum is an extra **node colour**, so Plan
41's colour-refinement canonical form extends to flavoured quivers unchanged:
`quiver_enumeration.canonical_form` now takes `colours=`, and a flavoured entry
is the pair *(canonical exchange matrix, node colours in canonical order)*
(`FlavouredQuiver.canonical_key`).  A node's colour is the multiset of
`(N_i, λ)` over the simple factors it is charged under — the factor's **rank**,
never its index in the product, because an isomorphism of flavoured quivers is
a node permutation *together with* an automorphism of `G` permuting its
factors.  Naming a factor by position gets two things wrong, and both were
caught: relabelling the nodes then changes the key, and `A1 × A1` with an
`SU(2)` at each node reports no symmetry although the two nodes are plainly
interchangeable.

| statement | status |
|---|---|
| relabelling the nodes leaves the key unchanged | checked |
| changing the flavour assignment changes the key | checked, 8 assignments on `A2` |
| `\|Aut\|` drops exactly when the flavour breaks a symmetry of `B` | checked (`A1×A1`: 2, 2, 1, 1 for unflavoured, `SU(2)²`, `SU(2)×1`, `SU(2)×SU(3)`) |
| **equal key ⇒ equal `S`** (dictionary soundness) | checked over a 20-quiver family with relabelled copies |
| the key is **strictly finer** than `S` | §9b |

**Not built, and refused rather than faked:** when a simple factor is charged
under by more than one node the node colours are *not* a complete invariant —
two nodes in the fundamental of one `SU(2)` and two nodes in fundamentals of
`SU(2) × SU(2)` have identical node colours and are different objects.
Separating them needs the node–factor incidence canonicalised jointly with the
quiver.  `canonical_key` honest-fails there.  The case does not arise in the
setting the construction is for, where each group of identical nodes has its
own factor.

## 9b. `S(B) = S(−B)`: the key is finer than `S`, and why

Distinct entries can share an `S`, and the reason is not about flavour at all.
Reversing every arrow, `B ↦ −B`, gives the opposite quiver.  The map
`X_γ ↦ X_γ` is an **anti**-isomorphism `Q_\fq(B) → Q_\fq(−B)`, so it carries a
product of `E^{(s)}` factors to the same factors in the **reversed** order —
and `S` is order-independent.  Hence

> **arrow-reversal invariance of `S` is a corollary of the PBW conjecture**,

and equivalently a testable prediction of it.  Measured unflavoured on 12
quivers with `B` genuinely not isomorphic to `−B`: `S(B) = S(−B)` on all 12.
Flavoured, the colouring makes `(B, colours)` and `(−B, colours)` distinct
entries which nevertheless share an `S` — so a dictionary keyed by flavoured
quivers is finer than one keyed by `S`, exactly as the unflavoured dictionary
is (the repo already folds enumerations by this sign flip — `restructuring_plans/
35_bps_atlas/catalogue_tame_novel.md`, `tests/test_bps_atlas_tame_novel.py` —
and the class-S Yin/Yang types are sign-related, Plan 11 `decisions.md`; what
does not appear to be recorded anywhere is that `S` itself is invariant under
it, or that this follows from order-independence).

*This section is the `SU(2)` tier, which is the largest and the one the
measurements below are taken on; §9d is the rest of the family.*

`dictionaries/flavoured/` (builder `dictionaries/build_flavoured.py`, tier
README beside the shards).  The user's specification, 2026-09-14: *"Code a
flavoured version of the BPS quiver dictionary.  For example, I could specify
`SU(2)` and expect a dictionary of quivers which have exactly one node doublet,
with `SU(2)`-covariant specs when available and otherwise `SU(2)`-covariant
crystal `S`'s"*.

**An entry is a quiver with a node doublet** — a transposition of two nodes
that is an automorphism of `B`, the two not pairing with each other, so they are
identical and permutable and the theory carries a manifest `SU(2)`.  The `B[i][j]
= 0` condition is part of the definition and not an optimisation: the two doublet
weights must span an isotropic direction for the flavour weight lattice to sit in
`ker B` as a factor of the charge lattice.  The key is the **unfolded** quiver in
canonical form, which makes the dictionary a *refinement* of
`dictionaries/enumerated/` whose membership is checkable against a set built by
a different route.

**Shipped: the `SU(2)` set (`doublets = 1`), complete through total arrow weight
`≤ 9` plus four complete weight-10 cells** — 156,225 quivers, **856 KB gzipped**
over 36 shards (723 KB for weight `≤ 9`, 133 KB for the weight-10 cells).  **The weight and the rank are the UNFOLDED quiver's**, which is what
makes the cells line up with the unflavoured tier; unfolding duplicates the
doublet node, so `e_unfolded = e_folded + d` with `d` that node's degree.

| certification | entries |
|---|---:|
| `covariant_spec:strip` — acyclic, the construction (coded, not stored) | 128,703 |
| `covariant_spec:blocks` — the block-mutation bidirectional BFS | 24,585 |
| `covariant_spec:order@5` — the order search, confirmed to degree 5 | 2,649 |

| `crystalline_failed@D` — `Ω` not a character at some charge | **0** |

Two things to read off this table, and one not to.

* **The emergent claim of §3 holds on 156,225 quivers.**  `Ω` being a
  `G`-character is never imposed anywhere in the recursion, and a quiver where
  it failed would be recorded as `crystalline_failed@D` — loudly, never dropped.
  The count is zero.  That is the largest body of evidence the flavoured
  axiomatics has.
* **The acyclic half is a theorem, not a search** (§6b, and the user's
  *"for acyclic it is easy to write an `SU(2)` covariant spec, by placing the
  two identical nodes consecutive"*, 2026-09-14): the two doublet nodes are
  identical and non-adjacent, so they can always be moved next to each other
  without disturbing a source/sink order, and consecutive is exactly what makes
  the two weight factors group into one flavoured generator.  Those entries are
  therefore **coded rather than stored** and recomputed on lookup — 84 % of the
  set, the `[A_1, D_3]` anchor among them, so a scan that forgets
  `include_acyclic=True` misses them.
* **What not to read off it:** the 288 `crystalline_only@3` entries are *not*
  evidence that no covariant spec exists there.  They are entries where neither
  finder reached one within its budget, which is a statement about the search,
  not about the quiver.  The rate is 0.24 % at weight 9 (53 in 22,382) and 4.6 %
  in the weight-10 cells (235 in 5,140), so it is a budget that bites harder as
  the quivers grow.
* **The dictionary now reaches PAST the unflavoured one.**  `dictionaries/enumerated/`
  ships to `E = 9`, so from weight 10 the refinement statement — every entry is in
  that set, checked against a route sharing nothing with this one — no longer
  applies.  The verifier skips it above `E` and reports how many it skipped,
  rather than reading an absence as a failure.
* **Only weight `≤ 9` is claimed complete.**  The manifest states that and marks
  weight 10 PARTIAL, listing exactly which of its cells are present.  Continuing
  the build is `restructuring_plans/41_enumerated_quiver_dictionary/flavoured_local_machine.md`,
  which carries the measured cost curve.

**How the shipped data is checked.**  `experiments/flavoured_dictionary_shipped_check.py`
re-checks every stored entry — **22,382 / 22,382 at weight 9 and 5,140 / 5,140
on the weight-10 cells, depth 4, 0 failures** (2026-09-15/16) — the fold against the recorded unfolded quiver,
membership in the unflavoured dictionary, and the spec rebuilding `S` on a cone
**strictly wider** than the search's, since an in-cone rebuild is a screen and
not a certificate (`repo_audit.md` A53/A56: three fixed depths have been
refuted, so the verdict is relative to the depth paid and the depth is what to
read).  The `[A_1, D_3]` anchor runs first as a positive control and the run
aborts if it fails.  A sample runs in CI, in
`tests/test_build_flavoured.py::test_committed_shards`.


## 9d. The rest of the family — `SU(N)` and the products

The user asked for the tier by flavour group, in four steps: *"an `SU(3)`
dictionary with the same structure (quivers with 3 identical nodes)"*, then
*"`SU(4)` and `SU(2) x SU(2)`"*, then *"`SU(5)` and `SU(3) x SU(2)`"*
(2026-09-16), with the correction that fixed the selector: *"not three pairs of
identical nodes, which would be `SU(2)^3`"*.

**The selector is the node ORBIT STRUCTURE.**  `node_orbits(B, N)` returns the
sets of `N` pairwise non-paired nodes every permutation of which is an
automorphism of `B` — `N` identical, interchangeable nodes — and
`node_orbit_families(B, sizes)` returns the families of pairwise *disjoint*
orbits of the given sizes.  Three objects that a doublet count would confuse,
and which the orbit selector separates:

* **one orbit of size 3** — three mutually identical nodes.  It *contains*
  three overlapping doublet pairs, so a `--doublets 3` selection would have
  returned a different set entirely.  This is `SU(3)`.
* **three disjoint orbits of size 2** — three separate pairs, no two of them
  interchangeable across pairs.  This is `SU(2)³`.
* **one orbit of size 4** — four mutually identical nodes.  These admit three
  ways of splitting into two pairs, so `node_orbit_families(B, [2, 2])` has
  three elements there and the quiver is *rejected* from the `SU(2)×SU(2)`
  tier rather than filed in it: that tier's acceptance is exactly one family.

The products needed one piece of machinery the single-orbit builder lacked.  Its
entry builder folded only the **first** orbit (`_entry_for(B, d[0], …)`), which
is correct when there is only one; with a family it produced entries carrying
one factor and the rest unfolded.  `fold_orbits(B, family)` folds a whole family

* **The emergent claim of §3 now holds across the whole family.**  `Ω` being a
  `G`-character is never imposed anywhere in the recursion, and a quiver where
  it failed would be recorded as `crystalline_failed@D`.  The count is zero in
  every tier, and the product tiers test it where `G` is not simple — `Ω` takes
  values in `R(SU(a) × SU(b)) ⊗ P`, so the multiplicities have to assemble into
  characters of a product, factor by factor, which is a strictly stronger
  condition than either factor alone.
* **The tiers shrink fast, and the reason is the weight law.**  Unfolding an
  orbit of size `N` at a node of degree `d` adds `(N−1)·d` to the unfolded
  weight, so a tier with orbits of sizes `{N_a}` at weight `≤ E` folds to weight
  `≤ E − Σ_a (N_a − 1)`.  That exponent is what the fold walk exploits and what
  makes `SU(5)` (500 quivers) cheap where `SU(2)` (156,225) is not.
* **The crystalline rate falls with the group**, and the mechanism is the same
  weight law.  1.0 % of stored entries at `SU(2)` (288 in 27,522), 0.09 % at
  `SU(3)` (2 in 2,311), and none at all in the other four.  The spec search
  runs on the **fold**, which has `Σ_a (N_a − 1)` fewer nodes than the entry's
  own quiver, so at a fixed unfolded weight a larger orbit hands the
  block-mutation BFS a strictly smaller problem.  Read it as a statement about
  the search budget, not about the quivers: a `crystalline_only@D` entry is one
  where neither finder reached a covariant spec within budget, which is never
  evidence that none exists.

covers the family in `tests/test_build_flavoured.py` —
`test_every_shipped_flavoured_tier` (census, certifications, the `factors`
assertion above, and a sampled spec rebuild on each tier) and
`test_a_tier_names_its_flavour_group_the_way_a_reader_would`.

## 10. The flavoured F-finder and the flavoured spec (user, 2026-09-14)

**`F·S = μ^w X_γ + O(\fq)`** on the reduced cone, with flavour-weight-graded
coefficients and the flavoured `S` as input — `flavoured_f_solver.py`.  The
relation is the repo's own (`kalgebra.md` "F-finding"); flavour changes the ring
the coefficients live in, not the relation.  Two things are worth stating:

* **the forcing is closed-form, not a linear solve.**  The `ζ = 0` term
  contributes `f` itself (`S_0 = 1`) and every other term sits at a strictly
  lower reduced charge, so *no `\fq^{≤0}` part* plus bar-invariance give
  `f_j = −r_j` for `j ≤ 0`, extended palindromically.  Triangular in the cone
  order, which is what makes `F` unique given the seed `f_{(γ,w)} = 1`;
* **the support is an output.**  The unflavoured solver takes the doubly-tropical
  interval as an exact window, which needs `σ`; this one assumes no `σ` on the
  flavoured side and solves to the cutoff, so agreeing with the unfolded chart's
  support is evidence rather than construction.

Measured: trivial flavour reproduces `BPSKAlgebra.F_qn` bit-for-bit; and against
the unfolded chart's own solver — which shares the defining relation and nothing
else, using the tropical window and `[n]_q` peeling — every node charge on four
flavoured quivers and **62/62 composite charges**.

The projection `(enlarged charge) ↦ (reduced charge, flavour weight)` is a
**bijection** (the ranks match, `Σ_a dim r_a = g + Σ_a (N_a − 1)`), so the two
labellings are interchangeable; `enlarged_charge` inverts it and refuses a
`(γ, w)` failing the N-ality condition rather than rounding.

### The flavoured spec, and the consecutiveness condition

User: *"a spec finder which finds specs of the flavoured form, where factors for
the same irrep are consecutive"*.  `flavoured_spec.py`.

A flavoured spec is an ordered finite sequence of `(γ_i, r_i)` with
`S = ∏_i E^{(0;r_i)}_\fq(X_{γ_i})`, spin `0` and unit exponents.  Since
`E^{(0;r)}_\fq(X_γ) = ∏_{w∈r} E^{(0)}_\fq(μ^w X_γ)`, **a flavoured spec is
exactly an unfolded spec whose same-irrep factors are consecutive** — the
condition is not an extra demand on top of "is a spec", it is what lets an
unfolded spec descend to `\cE_G` at all.

| statement | status |
|---|---|
| acyclic ⇒ the strip order gives the spec, one generator per node with its own rep | measured on the whole corpus |
| a spec is accepted only on a **strictly wider** cone, verdict relative to `confirmed_to` | enforced (`repo_audit.md` A53/A56 — three fixed depths were refuted) |
| the finder reaches a quiver where the strip leaves a core | measured: the 3-cycle, found in a random order, 4 generators |
| unfolding a flavoured spec gives consecutive blocks, and grouping recovers it | round-trips on the corpus |
| separating a rep's factors by a **non-commuting** factor breaks the grouping | measured, both tests refuse |

**One honest distinction, measured rather than assumed.**  Literal
consecutiveness is a property of the *presentation*, not an invariant of the
spec: factors at gauge charges with `⟨γ,γ'⟩ = 0` commute, so an intervening
commuting factor can be slid out without changing the element.  On `A1 × A1`
with an `SU(2)` at each node — where everything commutes — interleaving the two
generators leaves the product **equal to `S`**, while the literal grouping
refuses it.  So `flavoured_spec` ships both: `group_unfolded_spec` (the literal
condition, exactly as stated) and `group_unfolded_spec_up_to_commuting` (the
invariant question).  The latter is **sufficient, not complete** — the sequence
lives in a trace monoid and deciding reachability there can need moves its
greedy gather does not make — so a `None` from it means *"not grouped by this
procedure"*, never *"does not descend"*.


### The bidirectional BFS over block mutations (user, 2026-09-14)

*"We should have a bidirectional flavoured BFS for specs"*.  The cluster-side
finder is `BPSQuiver.find_negating_sequence`, the bidirectional BFS for a
maximal green sequence that CLAUDE.md keeps as *the cluster definition of the DT
invariant*.  Its flavoured counterpart changes the **move set**, not the search:
a move is a **block mutation** `μ_a = ∏_{i ∈ group a} μ_i`, mutating every
unfolded node of one flavoured node at once.

**The block move is well defined**, and for a reason rather than by fiat: inside
a group the unfolded nodes carry no arrows between them, so the charge shift
`max(0, −exchange[j][i])` vanishes and a node's blockmates never move its charge.
The internal order is therefore irrelevant (measured, all permutations, three
quivers), and the positive-cone admissibility can be tested once per block.  This
is the same commuting fact that makes the consecutiveness condition well posed —
here it appears on the mutation side.

Consequently a block-move negating sequence replays to `dim r_a` charges per
move, **consecutive by construction** — so the search is over flavoured specs
directly, not over unfolded specs to be filtered afterwards.

| statement | status |
|---|---|
| trivial `G` ⇒ the repo's own negating sequence | positive control, 4 quivers, lengths equal |
| a block sequence replays to a flavoured spec reproducing `S` | 7 quivers incl. `SU(4)` and the 3-cycle, confirmed one cone wider |
| bidirectional agrees with the plain forward BFS | same length on every case; both give valid specs |
| the two spec finders agree in length | **independent** — one searches green sequences by block mutation, the other the order on `Γ_+ × ½N × Irr(G)`; 5 quivers |

**Cost, measured rather than assumed.**  Bidirectionality buys 4–8× on the
larger quivers (`A5`/`A6` chains, `A5` with `SU(3)`, the 4-cycle) and is *0.7×* —
slightly slower — on the smallest, where the meet-in-the-middle overhead
dominates.  Both routes return the same length throughout.


## 11. `GBPSKAlgebra` — the flavoured chart as a class (user, 2026-09-14)

Name ruled by the user (*"I think a GBPSKAlgebra class would be fine"*), over the
assistant's recorded objection that the `(G,N)` tier settled the same question
the other way (no per-theory subclasses).  ⚠ The letter `G` now carries two
meanings across tiers: the **gauge** group in `PureGAbeKAlgebra` / `GNAbeKAlgebra`,
the **flavour** group here, following the user's own `\cE_G`.

**The labels are `(γ, r)`** — a reduced gauge charge and an irrep — with

    L_{(γ,r)}  =  Σ_{w ∈ r}  L_{(γ, w)}

over the weight diagram, the `L_{(γ,w)}` being the unfolded chart's canonicals.
That this, rather than `(γ, w)`, is the right index set was **measured on the
anchor**: the pre-2026-09-23 `A1DnKAlg`'s `L_{(0,1,0)}² = L_{(0,1,1)} + L_{(0,2,0)}`
is a Clebsch–Gordan sum where the unfolded chart's square is a single term — so
a flavoured basis element carries a whole rep.  (In the rebuilt class the same
doublet is `curve(0, 2, kappa=1)`, and its square is the Clebsch–Gordan pair
`L_{((0,2)², κ=0)} + L_{((0,2)², κ=2)}`; `tests/test_a1dn_kalg.py`.)  **N-ality** decides which irreps
live at a charge, and falls out of `enlarged_charge` rather than being imposed.

**The acceptance test is `A1DnKAlg`.**  `FlavouredQuiver([[0,1],[-1,0]], [1,2])`
unfolds to exactly its `n = 3` pairing, so the general machine must reproduce
the hand-written one-`SU(2)` instance — and does, **64/64** on a full product
table, with the irreps fusing.  *(Since 2026-09-23 `A1DnKAlg(3)` is the rebuilt
class on the curves of the triangle with one interior puncture; through a
test-local table of the six curves' gauge charges the acceptance test now
covers 576 products — crossing pairs included — and the traces and `ρ` as well,
all equal; `tests/test_gbps_kalgebra.py`.)*

|---|---|

**Native vs transported, stated plainly.**  Native — and the reason the class

afterwards by un-branching a Cartan presentation (`flavour_enhancement`).
Transported are `multiply`, `ρ` and `trace`, computed by expanding onto the
unfolded chart and collecting back.  That is legitimate for a measured reason —
the unfolded `σ`, `F` and structure constants are equivariant (115/115), so they
carry whole reps to whole reps — and it is the pattern `SkeinKAlgebra` already
uses.  The independent flavoured engines cross-check what they cover: `S`, `F`
and the spec.

### A finding about `A1DnKAlg` (`repo_audit.md` A81) — fixed 2026-09-23

The two **disagree on `ρ`** wherever the trivalent-node charge is non-zero.
`A1DnKAlg` documents the antipode `γ ↦ −γ`; the chart's half-monodromy `σ` is
different there.  **The trace axiom decides**, since `ρ` is not merely *an*
automorphism but the one pinned by `I_{a,b} = Tr(ρ(a)·b) = δ + O(\fq)` — and it
decides for `σ`: leading coefficient `1` at `(1,0,0)`, `(0,1,0)`, `(1,1,0)`,
`(1,1,1)`, against `𝖖²`, `0` and `𝖖⁴` for the antipode.

Why nothing caught it is the instructive part: the antipode **passes** every
verifier `A1DnKAlg` ships (`verify_rho_fixes_identity`, `verify_rho_inverse`,
`verify_rho_is_automorphism`, 25/25) because it genuinely is an automorphism,
and the one verifier that separates the canonical `ρ` from the others —
orthonormality — needs a trace, which that module does not implement.  A
primitive can satisfy every check a module ships and still be wrong, when the
check that pins it is the one the module cannot run.

**Not fixed here**: `A1DnKAlg` feeds `su2a1d3_gauged`, the skein tier and two
`BPSAtlas` suites this session did not study, and `ρ` is a contract primitive —
the user is the arbiter.

*Resolved 2026-09-23 (Plan 42 `decisions.md` R18, R19).*  The class with the
antipode was the `D_n`-chamber quantum torus (now `legacy/a1dn_kalg_chamber_torus.py`).  The
rebuilt `A1DnKAlg` — curves of the once-punctured `n`-gon, `ρ` the rotation
`x ↦ x + 1` — agrees with the chart's half-monodromy on every label tested, and
its trace makes orthonormality runnable (it passes).  Correction to the
paragraph above: the only importers were `su2a1d3_gauged.det1_certify` and the
tests, not the skein tier or `BPSAtlas` suites.

\section{How to read this document}

\subsection{Three verbs}
The paper defines structures; the repository does three things with each
of them.  It \emph{implements} the structure --- an abstract base class
whose primitives are the axioms, or a module whose functions are the
formulas.  It \emph{generates} instances --- constructors, solvers and
dictionaries that produce concrete algebras, flows and spectrum
generators.  And it \emph{tests} --- verifiers on the classes, test
suites under \file{tests/}, and dated measurement batteries.  Each
structure below gets one block with these three rows and a fourth,
\textsc{standing}, saying how much the evidence proves.

\subsection{The four standings}
A green check is not one kind of thing, and the source repository's
audit record (``the enforced-vs-emergent map'') separates them.
Every claim in this document carries one of four words.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.6cm,style=nextline]
  \item[\DERIVED] A theorem, with its derivation recorded in a design note
    of the repository (a file named in the block).  The code then
    \emph{certifies} the derived identity rather than discovering it.
  \item[\CERTIFIED] An exact identity checked by code, in exact arithmetic
    over $\bZ[\fq,\fq^{-1}]$ or the Habiro ring, on every case the named
    test enumerates.  Where the identity is built into the construction
    (``enforced''), the check is a regression guard; where it is
    independent of the construction (``emergent''), it is evidence.  The
    blocks say which.
  \item[\MEASURED] True on every case examined, with no derivation; the
    block names the battery and its size.
  \item[\OPEN] A conjecture of the paper that the repository tests but does
    not settle, or a structure of the paper with no counterpart in code.
\end{description}

\subsection{Conventions}
The code writes \code{q} for the paper's $\fq$; there is one quantum
parameter throughout, and the classical $q$ of the Schur-index literature
is $\fq^2$.  Laurent polynomials over $\bZ[\fq^{\pm1}]$ are
\code{LaurentPoly}, truncated power series over a flavour ring are
\code{RPowerSeries}, elements of the Habiro ring are \code{HabiroElement}.
Canonical basis elements are addressed by opaque Python labels; the
automorphism $\rho$ is \code{rho}, its inverse \code{rho_inverse}, the bar
involution \code{bar}, the trace \code{trace}, the pairing
$I_{a,b}=\Tr L_{\rho(a)}L_b$ is \code{inner_product}.  A ``chart'' is the
image of a canonical basis element in the ambient algebra of a
presentation (a quantum torus, a rational quantum torus, a cone).  All
structure constants, traces and pairings are computed exactly and
truncated only when printed; this is a discipline of the repository, not
a convenience, because both central relations of the paper end in
$O(\fq)$ and early truncation destroys the cancellations they rest on.

\section{$K_\fq$-algebras (Section~\pref{sec:kq} of the paper)}

\subsection{The definition as an abstract base class}
Definition~\pref{def:kq} is the class \code{KAlgebra} in
\file{src/core/kalgebra.py}: an associative algebra over $\bZ[\fq,\fq^{-1}]$ with
a canonical basis, presented through six abstract primitives ---
\code{coefficient_ring} (the flavour ring $R$, the integers when
unflavoured), \code{identity}, \code{multiply} (the structure constants
$C^c_{ab}(\fq)$ as \code{LaurentPoly}), \code{rho}, \code{rho_inverse}
and \code{trace} (into $R((\fq))$, truncated at a requested order) ---
plus one optional primitive for flavour (\S\ref{sec:flavoured}).  The
axioms are verifier methods; a subclass implements the primitives and
inherits the verifiers, the pairing, the linear extensions and the
flavour-ring change.

K4 is written in the paper in two forms, $\Tr L_{\rho(a)}L_b$ and
$\Tr L_bL_{\rho^{-1}(a)}$; the second is why $\rho^{-1}$ is a primitive
and not a derived inverse, and \code{verify_trace_pairing_faces} checks
that the two faces agree.  The pairing \code{inner_product} defaults to
multiply-then-trace and may be overridden by a presentation that computes
it more directly; \code{verify_inner_product_consistent} then compares the
override with the default, which is genuine evidence rather than a
tautology.

\subsubsection{The $\mathfrak u(1)$-gauged odd polygons}\label{comp:u1a1aodd}
For $k\ge1$ let $T$ be the $\mathfrak u(1)$-gauging of the
Argyres--Douglas theory $[A_1,A_{2k+1}]$: its BPS quiver is the linear
$A_{2k+2}$ chain with the last node frozen, and the polygon has
$H=2k+4$ vertices, one more than the $(2k+3)$-gon of the ungauged even
sibling $[A_1,A_{2k}]$ of Appendix~\pref{app:a1a2k}.

\paragraph{Labels.}  The canonical basis is indexed by cone monomials in
the diagonals of the $H$-gon together with an invertible generator $E$
(the gauge direction): letters
\begin{equation}
  L_{t;\,i} = \text{chord with endpoints }(i,\ i+t+1)\bmod H,
  \qquad t\in\{1,\dots,k+1\},\ i\in\bZ/H,
\end{equation}
the diameter $t=k+1$ satisfying $L_{k+1;\,i}=L_{k+1;\,i+H/2}$, edges
identified with $1$, and $E^{\pm1}$ adjoined as Laurent generators; a
native label is (non-crossing chord multiset, $e_E\in\bZ$).  Because $H$
is even, chord endpoint parity is rotation-invariant: the chords split
into balanced ($t$ even) and unbalanced ($t$ odd) families, and the
polygon has honest diameters.

\paragraph{$\rho$'s $E$-drift.}  $\rho$ rotates the polygon by one vertex
and inverts the gauge direction, but the image of a chord letter is the
rotated letter \emph{times an integer power of $E$} --- its \emph{drift}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-drift}
  \rho(L_{t;\,i}) = E^{\,d(t,i)}\,L_{t;\,i+1},\qquad \rho(E)=E^{-1},
  \qquad
  d(t,i)=\begin{cases}
    +2 & t=1,\ i=H-1,\\
    -2 & t\text{ odd},\ 3\le t\le k,\ i=H-t-1,\\
    +1 & t=k+1\text{ (the diameter)},\ i=H/2-1,\\
    \ \ 0 & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The drift vanishes except at one position in each chord type's orbit, so
going once around the orbit returns the same chord multiplied by a power of
$E$, with alternating signs since $\rho(E)=E^{-1}$: $\rho$ has honest order
$H$ on the balanced chords, order $H$ modulo $E^{\pm2}$ on the unbalanced
ones and order $H/2$ modulo $E$ on the diameter.  Its order is finite only
modulo the gauge generator --- the gauging costs conformality, and the order
of $\rho$ registers it; in the even family $\rho$ has honest order $2k+3$ on
every chord.

\paragraph{Charges and $\fq$-commutation.}  Each chord carries a charge
$\gamma$ in the $A_{2k+2}$-chain lattice $\bZ^n$, $n=2k+2$, with basis
$e_1,\dots,e_n$ and $\mu=e_1+e_3+\cdots$ the charge of $E$.  Writing the
chord as $\{u,v\}$ with $u=i$, $v=i+t+1$ unreduced, the non-wrapping
chords ($v\le H-1$) have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-charge}
  \gamma_{\{u,v\}}=-\!\!\sum_{\substack{n+2-v\le j\le n-1-u\\ j\equiv v\ (2)}}\!\! e_j
  \;-\;[\,u\equiv v\ (2)\,]\,\bigl(e_{n-u}-e_{n-u+1}+\cdots\pm e_n\bigr),
\end{equation}
one arithmetic progression of step two plus an alternating tail present
exactly for the odd-type chords; the wrapping chords ($v\ge H$) have
three closed-form shapes indexed by $w=i-(H-t-1)$ and $r=t-w$, plus a
single exception, an extra $-2\mu$ at $(t,w)=(1,1)$ --- the charge-level
face of the $E^2$ drift anchored at $(1,H-1)$ in \eqref{eq:comp-drift} (the wrap rows are
printed in the write-up and coded in \code{charge_formula}).  Two chords
$\fq$-commute iff the diagonals do not cross away from endpoints ($E$
$\fq$-commutes with every chord, by the same formula with $\gamma_E=\mu$:
the even types commute with $E$, the odd ones with a power $\fq^{\pm2}$), with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-qcomm}
  L_aL_b=\fq^{\,2\langle\gamma_a,\gamma_b\rangle}L_bL_a,\qquad
  \langle g,h\rangle=\textstyle\sum_i\bigl(g_ih_{i+1}-g_{i+1}h_i\bigr),
\end{equation}
and the canonical basis is the set of non-crossing monomials times
$E$-powers, normalised by the universal phase
$L_{\{a_1,\dots,a_m\}}=\fq^{-\sum_{i<j}c_{a_ia_j}}L_{a_1}\cdots L_{a_m}$
with $c_{ab}=\langle\gamma_a,\gamma_b\rangle$; the maximal cones are the
$\mathrm{Catalan}(2k+2)$ triangulations of the $H$-gon, each extended by
$E^{\pm1}$.

\paragraph{Quantum Ptolemy with $E$-dressing.}  Crossing chords obey a
two-term exchange relation whose monomials are the two resolutions of
the crossing quadrilateral, exactly as in the even family, each
resolution now carrying an integer $E$-power beside its $\fq$-power:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-ptolemy}
  L_{\overline{ac}}\,L_{\overline{bd}}
  =\fq^{\alpha}E^{\varepsilon_1}L_{\{\overline{ab},\overline{cd}\}}
  +\fq^{\beta}E^{\varepsilon_2}L_{\{\overline{ad},\overline{bc}\}},
\end{equation}
every coefficient being a unit monomial.  The exponents are given by
finite combinatorics of the same character as the even family's arc
rules: the $E$-exponents by charge bookkeeping, the $\fq$-exponents by
the even family's fewer-odd-arcs Ptolemy exponent on the merged
quadrilateral plus the torus pairing of the two factors' charges minus
the assembly exponent of the daughter's word.

\paragraph{The embedding into the even family.}  All relations are
generated by the even sibling's through one closed-form embedding, the
RG flow that un-gauges the $\mathfrak u(1)$ with a single quantum
dilogarithm as its spectrum generator:
\begin{equation}
  \Phi:\ A_\fq\hookrightarrow A^{\mathrm{even}}_\fq\otimes Q_\fq(\bZ^2),
  \qquad X_aX_b=\fq^{\langle a,b\rangle}X_{a+b},\quad\langle(1,0),(0,1)\rangle=1.
\end{equation}
Geometrically $\Phi$ is the vertex merge of the $H$-gon onto the
$(H-1)$-gon collapsing the edge $(H-1,0)$; write $\mathrm{ev}\{x,y\}$ for
the $(H-1)$-gon chord on the same vertices (edges $\mapsto1$).  For a
chord $\{u,v\}$, $0\le u<v\le H-1$, with $\ell=v-u$ and
$t=\min(\ell,H-\ell)-1$, set $c_0=(-1)^u$ for $t$ odd and $c_0=0$ for $t$
even.  Chords avoiding the merged vertex ($v\le H-2$) map to a single
term $\mathrm{ev}\{u,v\}\otimes X_{(c_0,c_1)}$ with $c_1=0$ for
$2\le\ell\le H/2$, $c_1=(-1)^u$ for $\ell>H/2$ and $t$ even, and, for
$\ell>H/2$ and $t$ odd, $c_1=+1$ at $u=0$ and $(-1)^{u+1}$ at $u\ge1$;
chords ending on the merged vertex ($v=H-1$) resolve both ways,
\begin{equation}
  \Phi(L_{\{u,H-1\}})=\mathrm{ev}\{u,0\}\otimes X_{(c_0,c_1^r)}
  +\mathrm{ev}\{u,H-2\}\otimes X_{(c_0,c_1^r-1)},\qquad
  c_1^r=\begin{cases}-1&u=1,\\ +1&2\le u\le k,\\ \ 0&u\ge k+1;\end{cases}
\end{equation}
and $\Phi(E^{\pm1})=X_{(0,\pm1)}$.  Every coefficient is exactly $1$.

\paragraph{The trace.}  With $p=k+2$ the chiral algebra is the rank-one
$M(1,p)$ singlet vertex algebra.  The $\rho^2$-twisted trace reduces by
cone-data cyclicity to the seeds $\Tr(E^n)$ and $\Tr(L_{t;i}E^n)$, with
$\Tr=0$ whenever the whole $\rho^2$-orbit of the seed is gauge-charged
(the odd-type chords carry $c_0=\pm1$ --- the same dichotomy as the drift
\eqref{eq:comp-drift} --- so their single powers vanish),
the vacuum tower being the singlet partial-theta characters
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-singlet}
  \Tr(E^n)=(-1)^{(p-1)|n|}\Bigl[\sum_{j\ge0}\fq^{\,2pj^2+(2p|n|+2p-2)j+(p-1)|n|}
  -\sum_{j\ge0}\fq^{\,2pj^2+(2p|n|+2p+2)j+(p+1)|n|+2}\Bigr],
\end{equation}
and every even-type chord following one rule.  Write $\hat\chi_{r,s}$ for the
false-theta numerator of the singlet module $M_{r,s}$ divided by its leading
power,
\begin{equation}
  \hat\chi_{r,s}=\sum_{j\ge0}\Bigl(\fq^{\,2pj^2+2j(pr-s)}-\fq^{\,2pj^2+2j(pr+s)+2rs}\Bigr),
  \qquad \hat\chi_{r,0}=0 .
\end{equation}
Then for the chord type $t=2m$ and every integer $n$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-longchord}
  \Tr(L_{2m;i}E^{e})=(-1)^{(p-1)n+m+1}\,\fq^{(p-1-2m)n-m}\,
  \bigl(\hat\chi_{n+1,m}-\hat\chi_{n+1,m+1}\bigr),
\end{equation}
with the character index $n=-(-1)^ig$, $g$ the seed's gauge charge (the
$e_1$-component of its total charge), for every type and every $k$ at the
positions $i=0,1$, one in each $\rho^2$-orbit of positions.  The other
positions follow from $\Tr\circ\rho^2=\Tr$: $\rho^2$ carries
$L_{2m;i}E^e$ to $L_{2m;i+2}E^{e+d(2m,i+1)-d(2m,i)}$ by \eqref{eq:comp-drift}.
Read at face value at another position, the index rule can fail (first at the
chord $\{2,5\}$ at $k=1$).  At $m=0$
this is the vacuum tower \eqref{eq:comp-singlet}; $m=1$ is the length-three
chord $L_{2;i}$ (a lattice-shifting Wilson line on the chiral algebra), and
$m=(p-1)/2$ the diameter at odd $k$.  At $n=0$ it reads
$(-1)^{m+1}\fq^{-m}(\hat\chi_{1,m}-\hat\chi_{1,m+1})$: the rule
$T_a=(-1)^{a+1}\fq^{-a}(\chi_a-\chi_{a+1})$ of the even family
(Appendix~\pref{app:a1a2k}) with singlet characters in place of the
$M(2,2k+3)$ ones.  At $k=1$ (the gauged hexagon, $p=3$): $\Tr(1)=1-\fq^2+\fq^{10}-\fq^{16}+\dots$,
$\Tr(E)=\fq^2-\fq^6+\dots$, $\Tr(E^2)=\fq^4-\fq^{10}+\dots$.

The rule \eqref{eq:comp-longchord} is measured, not derived.  Two exact
routes that share nothing but the algebra's axioms agree with it: the
orthonormality bootstrap trusting only \eqref{eq:comp-singlet} (through
$\fq^{84}$ at $p=4$ and $\fq^{64}$ at $p=5$, held out at $p=7$ through
$\fq^{42}$), and the trace transported through $\Phi$ into the even family
(through $\fq^{32}$--$\fq^{44}$ at $p=4,5,6$).  It replaced a four-partial-theta
form for $L_{2;i}$ printed here earlier, fitted at low order, which is wrong
from $\fq^{39}$ at $p=4$ (from $\fq^{43}$, $\fq^{63}$, $\fq^{59}$ at $p=5,6,7$);
through the cone-data cyclicity those deep errors had reached labels at
$\fq^{16}$.

\subsubsection{The ungauged odd polygons: $K_\fq([A_1,A_{2k+1}])$ with its $U(1)$ flavour}\label{comp:a1aodd}
The example above is the $\mathfrak u(1)$-gauging of the Argyres--Douglas
theory $[A_1,A_{2k+1}]$; this one is the theory itself, with its $U(1)$
flavour.  The repository presents it by ungauging the example above:
\code{ungauge_u1a1aodd(k)} is the subalgebra of \code{U1A1AoddKAlg(k)} of the
elements that commute with the gauge generator $E$ --- the centralizer of
$E$ --- in which $E$ becomes the flavour fugacity.  The notation of
\S\ref{comp:u1a1aodd} is kept: the $H$-gon, $H=2k+4$, its chords
$L_{t;\,i}=\{i,i+t+1\}$, their charges $\gamma_{t;\,i}\in\bZ^n$, $n=2k+2$, with
$\mu$ the charge of $E$, and $\rho$'s $E$-drift $d(t,i)$
\eqref{eq:comp-drift}.  At $k=1$ the theory is $[A_1,A_3]=[A_1,D_3]$, the
paper's Section~\pref{sec:a1d3}, whose $SU(2)$ flavour the class sees through
its Cartan $U(1)$.

\paragraph{Labels.}  $E$ $\fq$-commutes with every chord, by
\eqref{eq:comp-qcomm} with $\gamma_E=\mu$; the power is the chord's
\emph{magnetic charge}, twice the $e_n$-component of its charge, and by the
charge formulas it is set by the parities of the chord's two endpoints:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-aodd-mag}
  E\,L_{t;\,i}=\fq^{\,\mathrm{mag}(t,i)}\,L_{t;\,i}\,E,\qquad
  \mathrm{mag}(t,i)=2\langle\mu,\gamma_{t;\,i}\rangle=
  \begin{cases}
    -2 & i\text{ and }i+t+1\text{ both even},\\
    +2 & i\text{ and }i+t+1\text{ both odd},\\
    \ \ 0 & \text{one even, one odd}.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
So the magnetic charge vanishes exactly on the even types, the balanced
chords of \S\ref{comp:u1a1aodd}.  A canonical label $(F,e)$ of the gauged
algebra --- $F$ a multiset of pairwise non-crossing chords, $e$ the power of
$E$ --- commutes with $E$ iff the magnetic charges of $F$ add up to zero, that
is, iff $F$ is \emph{balanced}: it holds as many even--even diagonals as
odd--odd ones, counted with multiplicity.  The labels $(F,e)$ with $F$
balanced and $e\in\bZ$ are the canonical basis of the ungauged algebra, and
the multiset $F$ is its geometric label (\code{geometric_label}).  No labelling
on a polygon of the ungauged theory's own was found, and the author accepted
these multisets as the geometric labelling of the family.

\paragraph{The fugacity.}  Inside the centralizer $E$ is central, and the
class promotes it to the fugacity of the $U(1)$ flavour: the coefficient ring
is $R(U(1))=\bZ[z^{\pm1}]$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-aodd-lift}
  L_{(F,e)}=E^{e}\,L_{(F,0)}=z^{-e}\,L_{(F,0)},
\end{equation}
so the flavour-lift coordinate (\code{r_label_decompose}) sends $(F,e)$ to the
section $(F,0)$ and the weight $-e$, and the unflavoured canonical basis is the
set of $E$-free labels $(F,0)$.  The sign is the one the trace below obeys,
$\Tr L_{(F,e)}=z^{-e}\,\Tr L_{(F,0)}$.

\paragraph{Generators and relations.}  The multiplicative generators
(\code{mult_generators}) are the balanced chords $L_{2m;\,i}$, $k(k+2)$ of
them, and the pairs $L_{\{A,B\}}$ of one even--even diagonal $A$ and one
odd--odd diagonal $B$ that do not cross, $(k+2)\binom{k+2}{3}$ of them:
$6$, $24$ and $65$ generators at $k=1,2,3$.  The relations are those of
\S\ref{comp:u1a1aodd}, restricted: the product of two balanced labels is
computed in \code{U1A1AoddKAlg(k)} --- the $\fq$-commutation
\eqref{eq:comp-qcomm} and the quantum Ptolemy relation
\eqref{eq:comp-ptolemy} --- every term of it is balanced, since the magnetic
charge is additive, and each power $E^{\varepsilon}$ in it is the coefficient
$z^{-\varepsilon}$.  A label is the multiset, not a word in the generators:
from $k=3$ a triangulation can hold two even--even diagonals $A_1,A_2$ and two
odd--odd ones $B_1,B_2$, and then $L_{\{A_1,B_1\}}L_{\{A_2,B_2\}}$ and
$L_{\{A_1,B_2\}}L_{\{A_2,B_1\}}$ are single terms on the one label
$\{A_1,A_2,B_1,B_2\}$.

At $k=1$ the presentation is short enough to print in full, as the paper does
for $[A_1,D_3]$.  The generators are the three diameters
$L_{2;\,j}=\{j,j+3\}$ and the three pairs $L_{\{1;j,\,1;j+3\}}$ of the short
diagonals $\{j,j+2\}$ and $\{j+3,j+5\}$, $j\in\bZ/3$.  Each diameter
$\fq$-commutes with two pairs,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-aodd-hexqc}
  L_{2;\,j}L_{\{1;j,\,1;j+3\}}=\fq^{2}\,L_{\{1;j,\,1;j+3\}}L_{2;\,j},\qquad
  L_{2;\,j}L_{\{1;j+1,\,1;j+4\}}=\fq^{-2}\,L_{\{1;j+1,\,1;j+4\}}L_{2;\,j},
\end{equation}
and the canonical basis is the set of $z^n$ times the canonical monomials of
the six cones $\{L_{2;\,j},L_{\{1;j,\,1;j+3\}}\}$ and
$\{L_{2;\,j},L_{\{1;j+1,\,1;j+4\}}\}$.  The crossing products follow from
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-aodd-hex}
\begin{aligned}
  L_{2;\,0}L_{2;\,1}&=1+\fq^{-1}z\,L_{\{1;1,\,1;4\}},\\
  L_{2;\,1}L_{\{1;0,\,1;3\}}&=1+z+\fq\,L_{2;\,0}+\fq^{-1}L_{2;\,2},\\
  L_{\{1;0,\,1;3\}}L_{\{1;1,\,1;4\}}&=1+\fq^{-1}(1+z^{-1})\,L_{2;\,0}+\fq^{-2}z^{-1}L_{2;\,0}^{2},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
their bar images (the reversed products, with $\fq\mapsto\fq^{-1}$ and $z$
fixed) and $\rho$, \eqref{eq:comp-aodd-hexrho} below; with
\eqref{eq:comp-aodd-hexqc} they give all $36$ ordered products of the six
generators.

\paragraph{$\rho$.}  Ungauging keeps $\rho$ and turns its $E$-drift into a
power of the fugacity.  $\rho$ rotates every diagonal by one vertex, which
exchanges even--even and odd--odd diagonals and so maps balanced multisets to
balanced multisets, and it inverts $E$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-aodd-rho}
  \rho\bigl(L_{(F,e)}\bigr)=L_{(F',\,d(F)-e)}=z^{\,e-d(F)}\,L_{(F',0)},\qquad
  \rho(z)=z^{-1},
\end{equation}
with $F'$ the multiset of the rotated diagonals and $d(F)$ the sum of the
drifts \eqref{eq:comp-drift} of the chords of $F$, counted with multiplicity;
$\rho(z)=z^{-1}$ is F3 for $U(1)$.  At $k=1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-aodd-hexrho}
\begin{gathered}
  \rho(L_{2;\,j})=L_{2;\,j+1},\qquad
  \rho(L_{\{1;j,\,1;j+3\}})=L_{\{1;j+1,\,1;j+4\}}\qquad(j=0,1),\\
  \rho(L_{2;\,2})=z^{-1}L_{2;\,0},\qquad
  \rho(L_{\{1;2,\,1;5\}})=z^{-2}L_{\{1;0,\,1;3\}}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The gauged $\rho$ has finite order only modulo $E$; here $\rho^H$ is the
identity (measured on every generator at $k=1,2,3$).  Modulo powers of $z$ the
order of $\rho$ is $3$, $8$ and $10$ at $k=1,2,3$: the orders
Table~\pref{tab:finite_type} lists for $[A_1,A_3]=[A_1,D_3]$, $[A_1,A_5]$ and
$[A_1,A_7]$, and those of the $\rho$-permutations of the zoo's \code{a3},
\code{a5} and \code{a7}.

\paragraph{The trace.}  Ungauging a $U(1)$ restores its vector-multiplet
measure: the trace is the sum of the gauged traces graded by the gauge charge,
divided by $(\fq^2;\fq^2)_\infty^2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-aodd-trace}
  \Tr L_{(F,e)}=\frac{z^{-e}}{(\fq^2;\fq^2)_\infty^{2}}\sum_{n\in\bZ}z^{n}\,
  \Tr_{\mathrm{gauged}}\,L_{(F,n)},
\end{equation}
with $\Tr_{\mathrm{gauged}}$ the trace of \code{U1A1AoddKAlg(k)} and
$L_{(F,n)}=L_{(F,0)}E^{n}$.  The trace seeds are therefore the gauged ones:
each $\Tr_{\mathrm{gauged}}L_{(F,n)}$ reduces by the gauged cone-data
cyclicity to \eqref{eq:comp-singlet} and \eqref{eq:comp-longchord}.  The vacuum
and the balanced chords sum in closed form.  The chord $L_{2m;\,1}$ has gauge
charge $0$, so the character index of $L_{2m;\,1}E^{n}$ in
\eqref{eq:comp-longchord} is $n$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-aodd-closed}
  \Tr1=\frac{1}{(\fq^2;\fq^2)_\infty^{2}}\sum_{n\in\bZ}z^{n}\,\Tr_{\mathrm{gauged}}(E^{n}),\qquad
  \Tr L_{2m;\,1}=\frac{1}{(\fq^2;\fq^2)_\infty^{2}}\sum_{n\in\bZ}z^{n}\,
  \Tr_{\mathrm{gauged}}(L_{2m;\,1}E^{n}),
\end{equation}
with \eqref{eq:comp-singlet} and \eqref{eq:comp-longchord} at character index
$n$ inserted.  $\Tr L_{2m;\,0}$ is $\Tr L_{2m;\,1}$ with $z\mapsto z^{-1}$
($\rho(L_{2m;\,0})=L_{2m;\,1}$), and $\Tr L_{\rho^2(a)}=\Tr L_a$ gives the other
positions, $\Tr L_{2m;\,i+2}=z^{\,d(2m,i+1)-d(2m,i)}\,\Tr L_{2m;\,i}$; off the
diameter, $\Tr L_{2m;\,i}$ depends only on the parity of $i$.  The heads:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-aodd-heads}
\begin{aligned}
  k=1:&\quad \Tr1=1+(z^{-1}+1+z)\fq^2+(z^{-2}+2z^{-1}+3+2z+z^2)\fq^4+\dots,\\
  &\quad \Tr L_{2;\,0}=-(1+z)\fq-(z^{-1}+1+z+z^2)\fq^3+\dots,\\
  &\quad \Tr L_{\{1;0,\,1;3\}}=-z\fq+0\cdot\fq^3-(1+z+z^2)\fq^5+\dots,\\
  k=2:&\quad \Tr1=1+\fq^2-(z^{-1}+z)\fq^3+3\fq^4-2(z^{-1}+z)\fq^5+(z^{-2}+5+z^2)\fq^6+\dots,\\
  k=3:&\quad \Tr1=1+\fq^2+(z^{-1}+3+z)\fq^4+(2z^{-1}+5+2z)\fq^6+\dots.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
At $k=1$ these are the paper's $[A_1,D_3]$ traces \peqref{eq:a1d3trace}
restricted to the Cartan $U(1)$, $\chi_n\mapsto z^{-n/2}+z^{1-n/2}+\dots+z^{n/2}$:
$\Tr1$ is the restricted $\Tr1$, and $\Tr L_{2;\,0}=z^{1/2}\,\Tr D_0$ and
$\Tr L_{\{1;0,\,1;3\}}=z\,\Tr T_0$ after the restriction (through $\fq^{30}$
against \code{A1D3KAlg}).

\subsubsection{$K_\fq([A_1,D_4])$ with its $SU(3)$ flavour}\label{comp:su3ad}
The $[A_1,D_4]$ theory is the $D$-series member whose $SU(2)\times U(1)$
flavour is enhanced to $SU(3)$ by triality (Table~\pref{tab:finite_type}
lists it with $SU(3)$ and $\rho$ of order $4$).  The algebra has the
shape of $K_\fq([A_1,D_3])$ in Section~\pref{sec:a1d3} with $\bZ_3$
replaced by $\bZ_4$ and $R(SU(2))$ by $R(SU(3))$, whose characters are
written $\chi_{(p,q)}$ in Dynkin labels ($\chi_{(1,0)}$ and
$\chi_{(0,1)}$ the two fundamentals, $\chi_{(1,1)}$ the adjoint).
\begin{itemize}
\item The algebra is generated multiplicatively by eight canonical basis
  elements, four $T_i$ and four $D_i$, $i$ modulo $4$.
\item The defining relations, for $i$ even, are
\begin{align}
  T_iT_{i+1}&=1+\fq^{-1}\chi_{(0,1)}D_i+\fq^{-2}\chi_{(1,0)}D_i^2+\fq^{-3}D_i^3, &
  T_{i+1}T_i&=1+\fq\,\chi_{(0,1)}D_i+\fq^{2}\chi_{(1,0)}D_i^2+\fq^{3}D_i^3,\\
  D_iD_{i+1}&=1+\fq^{-1}T_{i+1}, & D_{i+1}D_i&=1+\fq\,T_{i+1},\\
  D_iD_{i+2}&=\fq^{-1}D_{i+1}+\chi_{(0,1)}+\fq\,D_{i-1}, \\
  T_iD_{i+1}&=\fq^{-2}D_i^2+\fq^{-1}\chi_{(1,0)}D_i+\chi_{(0,1)}+\fq\,D_{i-1}, &
  D_{i+1}T_i&=\fq^{-1}D_{i-1}+\chi_{(0,1)}+\fq\,\chi_{(1,0)}D_i+\fq^{2}D_i^2,\\
  T_iD_{i+2}&=\fq^{-1}D_i+\chi_{(1,0)}+\fq\,\chi_{(0,1)}D_{i-1}+\fq^{2}D_{i-1}^2, &
  D_{i+2}T_i&=\fq^{-2}D_{i-1}^2+\fq^{-1}\chi_{(0,1)}D_{i-1}+\chi_{(1,0)}+\fq\,D_i,
\end{align}
\begin{equation}
  T_iT_{i+2}=\fq^{-3}T_{i+1}+\fq^{-2}+\fq^{-1}\bigl(\chi_{(1,0)}D_{i+1}+\chi_{(0,1)}D_i\bigr)
  +2+\chi_{(1,1)}+\fq\bigl(\chi_{(0,1)}D_{i+2}+\chi_{(1,0)}D_{i-1}\bigr)+\fq^{2}+\fq^{3}T_{i-1},
\end{equation}
  together with the $\fq$-commutations $T_iD_i=\fq^{-2}D_iT_i$ and
  $T_iD_{i-1}=\fq^{2}D_{i-1}T_i$; for $i$ odd the same relations hold
  with every $\chi_{(p,q)}$ replaced by $\chi_{(q,p)}$.
\item The remaining canonical basis elements are the monomials
  $\fq^{ab}T_i^aD_i^b$ and $\fq^{-ab}T_i^aD_{i-1}^b$, dressed by the
  characters $\chi_{(p,q)}$.  Their geometric labels

  (\S\ref{comp:a1deven}) with the $SU(3)$ weight: $T_i$ is the loop at
  marked point $i$ with the curve $(i+1,2)$, $D_i$ the curve $(i+1,3)$, and
  a monomial the union of its letters' curves.
\item $\rho(T_i)=T_{i+1}$, $\rho(D_i)=D_{i+1}$ and $\rho(\chi_{(p,q)})=\chi_{(q,p)}$
  --- the twisted-linear F3 with $\star$ the conjugation $3\leftrightarrow\bar3$ ---
  generate the $\bZ_4$ symmetry; the alternation of $\chi$-labels between
  even and odd $i$ is this $\star$.
\item The trace is defined by the cyclicity axiom and three elementary
  traces, $\Tr1$, $\Tr T_0$ and $\Tr D_0$, all three closed forms of the
  family of \S\ref{comp:a1deven} at $k=1$.  Restricted to $SU(2)\times U(1)$
  this algebra is \code{A1DevenKAlg(1)}, with $1$, $T_0$, $D_0$ going to the
  labels $\emptyset$, $\{(0,4),(1,2)\}$, $\{(1,3)\}$
  (\S\ref{comp:a1deven}), and each elementary trace is the ungauged trace
  \eqref{eq:comp-deven-ungauge} of its image --- the sum over the gauge
  charge, with the measure restored, of Creutzig's gauge tower
  \eqref{eq:comp-deven-tower} for $\Tr1$ and of the seed closed forms
  \eqref{eq:comp-deven-seeds} for $\Tr T_0$ and $\Tr D_0$ --- in which a term
  $\chi_\kappa z^f$ stands for the $SU(2)\times U(1)$ weights of $U(1)$
  charge $3f+\Delta$, with $\Delta=0$ for $1$ and $T_0$ and $\Delta=1$ for
  $D_0$ (the restriction is injective on class functions):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-su3-traces}
\begin{aligned}
  \Tr1&=1+\chi_{(1,1)}\fq^2+\bigl(1+\chi_{(1,1)}+\chi_{(2,2)}\bigr)\fq^4
        +\bigl(1+\chi_{(0,3)}+2\chi_{(1,1)}+\chi_{(2,2)}+\chi_{(3,0)}+\chi_{(3,3)}\bigr)\fq^6+\dots,\\
  \Tr T_0&=-\fq-\chi_{(1,1)}\fq^5+\dots,\qquad
  \Tr D_0=-\chi_{(1,0)}\fq-\chi_{(2,1)}\fq^3-\bigl(\chi_{(0,2)}+\chi_{(1,0)}+\chi_{(2,1)}+\chi_{(3,2)}\bigr)\fq^5+\dots,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
  Two earlier routes are kept as independent witnesses.  $\Tr1$ is the
  Kac--Wakimoto vacuum character of $\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}(3)$ at level
  $-\tfrac32$ (the $[A_1,D_4]$ chiral algebra), equal to the served one

\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{$K_\fq([A_1,D_{2k+2}])$ and its $\mathfrak u(1)$ gauging: curves on the once-punctured polygon}\label{comp:a1deven}
The theories $[A_1,D_{2k+2}]$ have flavour $SU(2)\times U(1)$, enhanced to
$SU(3)$ at $k=1$, where the algebra is $K_\fq([A_1,D_4])$ of
\S\ref{comp:su3ad}.  The repository presents the family by two
self-contained classes, for every $k\ge1$: \code{U1A1DevenConeKAlgebra(k)},
the algebra of the theory with its $U(1)$ gauged, whose canonical basis is
labelled by curves on a polygon with one interior puncture; and
\code{A1DevenKAlg(k)}, the ungauged algebra, obtained from it as the
centraliser of the gauge generator.  The measurements quoted are at
$k=1,2,3$ unless stated.

\paragraph{Curves.}  Number the marked points of the $(2k+2)$-gon by
$\bZ/(2k+2)$ and put one puncture inside.  A curve $(x,\ell)$,
$2\le\ell\le2k+2$, runs from marked point $x$ to $x+\ell$ with $\ell$ boundary
edges on the side away from the puncture; $\ell=2k+2$ is the loop at $x$
around the puncture.  These are the curves of \code{A1DnKAlg}, on a polygon with an even number of marked points, and
there are $(2k+2)(2k+1)$ of them.  A curve has a magnetic charge
$c_0\in\{0,\pm1\}$: $c_0=0$ if $\ell$ is odd; if $\ell$ is even both endpoints
have one parity, and $c_0=-1$ if it is the parity of marked point~$2$,
$c_0=+1$ if not.  The maximal sets of pairwise non-crossing curves are the
$\binom{4k+2}{2k+1}$ sets of $2k+1$ curves ($20$, $252$, $3432$ at
$k=1,2,3$).

\paragraph{Labels.}  The gauged algebra has one more generator, invertible:
the gauge direction $E$ ($X_{0,1}$ in the code).  Its flavour ring is
$R(SU(2))$, and its canonical basis is labelled by triples $(F,e,\kappa)$ ---
$F$ a multiset of pairwise non-crossing curves, $e\in\bZ$ the power of $E$,
$\kappa\ge0$ the $SU(2)$ weight --- with $L_{(F,e,\kappa)}=\chi_\kappa
L_{(F,e,0)}$: the flavour is carried in the label, as in \code{A1DnKAlg}'s
$(\text{curves},\kappa)$.  The magnetic charge of a
label is $c_0(F)=\sum_{(c,m)\in F}m\,c_0(c)$.  The ungauged algebra
\code{A1DevenKAlg(k)} is the centraliser of $E$ --- the labels with
$c_0(F)=0$, so that $F$ is \emph{balanced}, with as many curves of charge
$+1$ as of charge $-1$ counted with multiplicity --- with $E$ promoted to the
fugacity $z$ of the $U(1)$ flavour, $E=z^{-1}$.  Its labels are the same
triples with $F$ balanced, over $R(SU(2))\otimes R(U(1))$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-deven-lift}
  L_{(F,e,\kappa)}=z^{-e}\chi_\kappa\,L_{(F,0,0)}.
\end{equation}
Its multiplicative generators are the curves of odd length and the
non-crossing pairs of a curve of charge $+1$ and one of charge $-1$ ---
$4+4$, $12+27$, $24+96$ at $k=1,2,3$ --- so some generators are pairs of
curves.  No labelling by curves on a polygon of the ungauged theory's own was
found; the balanced multiset is its geometric labelling.

\paragraph{Where $e$ is measured from.}  The power $e$ needs a reference
element for each curve.  The class takes it from the RG flow it was built
from, \code{U1A1DevenViaDoddRG(k)} (below): the flow's image of a curve has,
as its term of lowest power of $E$, with coefficient $1$, the curve of the
once-punctured $(2k+1)$-gon obtained by collapsing the boundary edge
$\{1,2\}$, and that element is $e=0$.  The edge $\{1,2\}$ therefore enters
three rules: (a)~in a product a piece of boundary is $1$, except along the
edge $\{1,2\}$, where it is $E$; (b)~$\rho$ costs one extra $E^{-1}$ per
endpoint at marked point~$1$; (c)~the parity of marked point~$2$ sets $c_0$.
The author fixed this convention.
No labelling avoids naming a position: a full turn of $\rho$ returns every
curve to itself times $E^{2c_0}$ \eqref{eq:comp-deven-rho}, a shift that does
not depend on where $e=0$ is put, so $e$ must jump somewhere on the way round.

\paragraph{Relations.}  $E$ $\fq$-commutes with every curve, and two curves
$\fq$-commute exactly when they do not cross:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-deven-qcomm}
  E\,L_c=\fq^{-2c_0(c)}L_c\,E,\qquad
  L_gL_h=\fq^{\,c(g,h)}L_{(\{g,h\},0,0)}\quad(g,h\text{ not crossing}),
\end{equation}
with $c$ antisymmetric and valued in $\{-1,0,1\}$.  It is read off the odd
family: $c(g,h)$ is the power of $\fq$ in the product $L_{\bar g}L_{\bar h}$
of \code{A1DnKAlg(2k+1)}, with $\bar g$, $\bar h$ the curves $g$, $h$
after collapsing the edge $\{1,2\}$ (marked points $v\ge2$ renumbered $v-1$),
and $c(g,h)=0$ when one of them becomes a boundary edge.  So the
canonical element of a label is the normalised monomial
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-deven-norm}
  L_{(F,e,\kappa)}=\chi_\kappa\,\fq^{-\sum_{i<j}c(a_i,a_j)-e\,c_0(F)}\,L_{a_1}\cdots L_{a_m}E^e,
\end{equation}
$a_1,\dots,a_m$ the curves of $F$ listed with multiplicity.  Two crossing
curves multiply by the skein resolutions of the odd family's model
(\file{src/cone/a1dodd_skein.py}), run on the doubled $2(2k+2)$-gon
with its half-turn, where a curve is a centrally symmetric pair of chords and
a loop a diameter:
\begin{itemize}
\item crossing once, neither a loop: the two Ptolemy resolutions;
\item crossing twice, neither a loop: the four states smoothing the two
  crossings, one of which carries $\chi_1$;
\item a loop and a curve: the two resolutions of the loop's diameter with the
  curve, and $\chi_1$ times the two curves joining the curve's endpoints to
  the loop's marked point, which lies on the curve's side away from the
  puncture;
\item two loops: $P^2+\chi_1PQ+Q^2$, with $P$, $Q$ the two reconnections of
  their diameters.
\end{itemize}
In each term a piece of boundary is $1$ except the edge $\{1,2\}$, which is
$E$, and a loop around the puncture is $\chi_1$.  Every coefficient is a
single power of $\fq$, times $\chi_1$ at most: $\fq^{\sum_fc(g,f)}$, summed
over the factors $f$ of the term ($E$ counted with its multiplicity,
$c(g,E)=c_0(g)$), for one crossing and for two loops, $g$ the left factor;
$\fq^{\pm\sum_fc(\lambda,f)}$ for a loop $\lambda$ and a curve, with $+$ when the
loop is the left factor; and $\fq^{A+1-\#B}$ for two crossings, with $\#B$ the
number of the two crossings smoothed the second way in the model's convention
and $A$ the exponent $\sum_fc(g,f)$ of the state carrying $\chi_1$.  At $k=1$,
one product of each kind:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-deven-products}
\begin{aligned}
  L_{(0,2)}L_{(1,2)}&=1+\fq^{-1}E\,L_{(0,3)}, &
  L_{(0,3)}L_{(2,3)}&=\fq^{-1}L_{(1,3)}+\fq\,L_{(3,3)}+\chi_1+E,\\
  L_{(0,2)}L_{(1,4)}&=\fq^{-2}E\,L_{(1,3)}+L_{(2,3)}+\fq^{-1}\chi_1E, &
  L_{(0,4)}L_{(1,4)}&=1+\fq^{-1}\chi_1L_{(1,3)}+\fq^{-2}L_{(1,3)}^2,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and $L_{(1,4)}L_{(0,2)}$ is the third with every power of $\fq$ inverted.

\paragraph{$\rho$.}  $\rho$ rotates the polygon by one marked point and
inverts $E$, the power of $E$ drifting at marked point~$1$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-deven-rho}
\begin{gathered}
  \rho\bigl(L_{(\{(x,\ell)\},e,\kappa)}\bigr)=L_{(\{(x+1,\ell)\},\,-e+d(x,\ell),\,\kappa)},\qquad
  \rho(E)=E^{-1},\\
  \rho^{2k+2}\bigl(L_{(\{c\},e,\kappa)}\bigr)=L_{(\{c\},\,e+2c_0(c),\,\kappa)},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
where $-d(x,\ell)$ is the number of endpoints of $(x,\ell)$ at marked
point~$1$, the loop at $1$ counting two; on a general label every curve
rotates and $e\mapsto-e+\sum_{(c,m)\in F}m\,d(c)$.  The last identity is the
full turn: $\rho$ has order $2k+2$ on the curves of odd length and order
$2k+2$ modulo $E^{\pm2}$ on the others.  On the ungauged algebra
\eqref{eq:comp-deven-lift} makes $\rho$ twisted-linear, $\rho(z)=z^{-1}$.

\paragraph{The trace.}  Write $p=k+1$, $\mu$ for the $SU(2)$ fugacity and
$\chi_r$ for the character of dimension $r+1$, as in \S\ref{comp:a1dodd}.
The trace of \code{U1A1DevenConeKAlgebra(k)} is fixed in four steps.
(i)~$\Tr L_{(F,e,\kappa)}=0$ unless $c_0(F)=0$.
(ii)~The powers of $E$, the gauge tower, are the $x^n$ slice of Creutzig's
$(A_1,D_{2p})$ index (arXiv:1701.05926, as \file{src/cone/exact_characters.py} cites
it) times the measure of the $U(1)$ vector multiplet:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-deven-tower}
  \Tr E^n=(-1)^{pn}\,\frac{\sum_{m\ge|n|+1,\ m\equiv n+1\ (2)}\fq^{\,p(m^2-n^2-1)/2}\,\chi_{m-1}}
  {\prod_{j\ge1}(1-\mu^2\fq^{2j})(1-\mu^{-2}\fq^{2j})}.
\end{equation}
The sign $(-1)^{pn}$ is the branch $q^{1/2}=-\fq$, $q=\fq^2$ the
literature's variable, with which the code imports the formula.
(iii)~The \emph{seeds} --- a curve of odd length, or a non-crossing pair of a
curve of charge $+1$ and one of charge $-1$, times $E^n$: one per generator
of \code{A1DevenKAlg(k)} --- have closed forms.  A seed is first carried by
$\rho$ to the representative of its orbit, the rotation whose sorted list of
curves is lexicographically smallest, with the power of $E$ that $\rho$ gives
it (the trace is $\rho$-invariant).  With
\[
  \Tr\bigl(L_FE^n\bigr)\,(\mu-\mu^{-1})\prod_{j\ge1}(1-\mu^2\fq^{2j})(1-\mu^{-2}\fq^{2j})
  =\sum_{e\ge1}c_e(n)\,(\mu^e-\mu^{-e}),
\]
and $\chi_r$ standing for $\chi_r(\fq^e)=\fq^{re}+\fq^{(r-2)e}+\dots+\fq^{-re}$
in the brackets below ($\chi_{-1}=0$), the rules for the three kinds of
representative are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-deven-seeds}
\begin{aligned}
  &\text{the curve }(0,2j+1)\text{:}\\
  &\quad c_e(n)=(-1)^{pn+p+j}\fq^{\,p(e^2-n^2)/2+(n-1)(p-j)}\bigl(\chi_j-\fq^{-n}\chi_{j-1}\bigr),\\
  &\text{a nested pair:}\\
  &\quad c_e(n)=(-1)^{pn+\frac{g_1-g_2}2}\fq^{\,p(e^2-n^2-1)/2+n\frac{g_1-g_2}2}
     \sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\fq^{2i}\bigl(\fq\chi_{r_i+1}+\fq^{-1}\chi_{r_i-1}-(\fq^n+\fq^{-n})\chi_{r_i}\bigr),\\
  &\text{a side-by-side pair:}\\
  &\quad c_e(n)=(-1)^{pn+\frac g2}\fq^{\,p(e^2-n^2)/2-\frac g2-2+n\frac{g_2-g_1}2}
     \chi_{a-1}\chi_{b-1}\bigl(\chi_1-\fq^n-\fq^{-n}\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
on the supports $e\ge|n-1|+1$, $e\equiv n$ (mod $2$) for the curve,
$e\ge|n|+1$, $e\equiv n+1$ for the nested pair, and $e\ge|n|$, $e\equiv n$ for
the side-by-side pair, with $c_e(n)=0$ off them.  (The gauge tower is the same
shape, $c_e(n)=(-1)^{pn}\fq^{p(e^2-n^2-1)/2}$ on the support of the nested
pair.)  Here $1\le j\le k$ for the curve.  A nested pair has the curve of
charge $-1$, of length $2j$, inside the other; $g_1$ and $g_2$ are the numbers
of marked points strictly between the two curves at their starts and at their
ends, and $r_i=\tfrac{g_1+g_2}2+1+2i$.  A side-by-side pair has the curve of
charge $-1$ of length $2a$ and the one of charge $+1$ of length $2b$, $g_1$
marked points strictly between the end of the first and the start of the
second, $g_2$ in the other gap, and $g=g_1+g_2$.  The loop around the puncture
enters as a curve of length $2k+2$, and the rules are uniform in $k$.
(iv)~Every other label is reduced to seeds and powers of $E$ by the cone
data's Layer-1 reduction --- $\rho^2$-twisted cyclicity and the product rule,
\code{ConeData.simplify_trace_via_cone_data}, the reducer of
\code{U1A1AoddKAlg} and of the zoo --- with coefficients in
$R(SU(2))[\fq^{\pm1}]$.  In every measurement its leaves are powers of $E$ and
single curves of odd length times powers of $E$: it takes the pairs apart
too, so every trace follows from \eqref{eq:comp-deven-tower} and the curve
rule, and each pair seed so reduced equals its own closed form (through
$\fq^{40}$ at $k=1,2$ and $\fq^{30}$ at $k=3$).  The pairing is
multiply-then-trace.

The seed rules are measured, not derived.  They were fitted on exact data
from the transport route below and then held out: against it $1{,}583$
evaluations at $k=1..5$ and $108$ at $k=6$ agree, none disagreeing, with every
evaluation at $k=5,6$ and every curve at $k\ge3$ held out from the fits.  The
side-by-side rule was corrected once: its first form, with $\fq^{\,ng/2}$ in
place of $\fq^{\,n(g_2-g_1)/2}$, was read at $k=2,3$, where $g_1=0$, and failed
at $k=4$.  The rules are recorded, with their witnesses, as the starting point of
a separate formal certification.

\paragraph{The ungauged trace.}  Ungauging restores the measure of the
$U(1)$ vector multiplet: the trace of \code{A1DevenKAlg(k)} is the sum over
the gauge charge of the gauged traces,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-deven-ungauge}
  \Tr L_{(F,e,\kappa)}=\frac{z^{-e}\chi_\kappa}{(\fq^2;\fq^2)^2_\infty}\sum_{n\in\bZ}z^n\,\Tr L_{(F,n,0)},
\end{equation}
the traces on the right those of \code{U1A1DevenConeKAlgebra(k)}, summed
through $\fq^K$ over $|n|\le K+1$; for a generator every term on the right is
a seed.  The heads of $\Tr1$ are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-deven-heads}
\begin{aligned}
  k=1\!:&\quad 1+\bigl(1+\chi_2+\chi_1(z+z^{-1})\bigr)\fq^2\\
   &\qquad+\bigl(3+2\chi_2+\chi_4+(2\chi_1+\chi_3)(z+z^{-1})+\chi_2(z^2+z^{-2})\bigr)\fq^4+\dots,\\
  k=2\!:&\quad 1+(1+\chi_2)\fq^2-\chi_1(z+z^{-1})\fq^3+(3+2\chi_2+\chi_4)\fq^4+\dots,\\
  k=3\!:&\quad 1+(1+\chi_2)\fq^2+\bigl(3+2\chi_2+\chi_4+\chi_1(z+z^{-1})\bigr)\fq^4+\dots
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The $\fq^2$ term is the flavour current, $1+\chi_2$, joined at $k=1$ by
$\chi_1(z+z^{-1})$: there it is the adjoint $\chi_{(1,1)}$ of
\eqref{eq:comp-su3-traces} restricted along $3\to2_{+1}\oplus1_{-2}$, with $z$
of $U(1)$ charge $3$.  The terms $\chi_1z^{\pm1}$ come from $\Tr E^{\pm1}$,
whose first term is $(-1)^p\fq^p\chi_1$ by \eqref{eq:comp-deven-tower}.

\paragraph{$k=1$: $K_\fq([A_1,D_4])$ and its $SU(3)$.}  Restricted to

\subsubsection{$K_\fq([A_1,D_5])$ and $K_\fq([A_1,D_7])$: the paper's $[A_1,D_3]$, continued}\label{comp:a1dodd}
\begin{sloppypar}
The paper's $K_\fq([A_1,D_3])$ (Section~\pref{sec:a1d3}) is the first member
of the family $[A_1,D_{2n+1}]$, whose flavour is $SU(2)$ and whose
elementary traces are characters of $\widehat{\fsl}(2)$ at the admissible
level $k=-4n/(2n+1)$: $-4/3$, $-8/5$ and $-12/7$ for $n=1,2,3$.  The
repository carries the next two members as self-contained cone algebras in
which every trace is an explicit combination of such characters.
\end{sloppypar}

\paragraph{Presentation.}  The generators are the cluster variables of the
$D_{2n+1}$ BPS quiver with the flavour folded into the coefficients --- $20$
for $D_5$ and $42$ for $D_7$, the generator $i$ carrying the label
$((i,1),)$ --- and the canonical basis is the set of cone monomials, as for
the pentagon.  The relations are the exported cone table: the cones (the
sets of pairwise $\fq$-commuting generators), the cocycle, and every product
of two generators that do not $\fq$-commute, written as an $\fq$-Laurent
combination of cone monomials with coefficients in $R(SU(2))$.  A complete
table of this kind is a presentation by generators and known relations.  $\rho$ permutes the generators, with
order $5$ and $7$ as Table~\pref{tab:finite_type} lists.  Both tables were
generated from the BPS charts.  An independent hand-written presentation
exists for each, and the two agree on every generator product the
hand-written one covers, once $\chi_nL_\gamma$ is matched with the
hand-written label that splits as $(\gamma,n)$.

\paragraph{Geometric labels.}  The whole family $[A_1,D_{2n+1}]$ also has a
self-contained class of its own, whose relations are closed-form exchange
relations on the curves of the once-punctured $(2n+1)$-gon with
coefficients in $R(SU(2))$.  The generator map from each exported table onto
it, with the flavour moved from the coefficients into the labels as the
family class carries it, is a certified isomorphism of $K_\fq$-algebras.
Through that map the canonical basis is labelled geometrically: a generator
is one curve $(x,\ell)$, running from the marked point $x\in\bZ/(2n+1)$ to
$x+\ell$ with $\ell$ boundary edges on the side away from the puncture,
$2\le\ell\le 2n+1$, the curve with $\ell=2n+1$ being the loop around the
puncture; these are $(2n+1)\cdot 2n$ curves, one for each of the $20$ and
$42$ generators.  A cone monomial is named by the multiset of its
generators' curves, and $\rho$ is the rotation $(x,\ell)\mapsto(x+1,\ell)$.
The map is fixed only up to a power of $\rho$, which rotates every label by
the same step.

\paragraph{The trace.}  As for $[A_1,D_3]$, cyclicity reduces every trace to
$\Tr1$ and one seed per $\rho^2$-orbit of generators: two seeds for $D_3$
($T$ and $D$), four for $D_5$ and six for $D_7$, the seed $s_i$ being the
generator $i$.  With $u=2$ and $v=2n+1$ (so $k+2=u/v$), $\mu$ the $SU(2)$
fugacity, $\chi_1=\mu+\mu^{-1}$ and $\bar f(\mu)=f(\mu^{-1})$, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-a1dodd-kappa}
\begin{gathered}
  \sigma_s=\sum_{j\in\bZ}\Bigl(\fq^{2uvj^2+(4us-2v)j}\mu^{2uj}
    -\fq^{2uvj^2+(4us-6v)j+2v-2us}\mu^{2uj-2}\Bigr),\\
  V=\prod_{m\ge1}\bigl[(1-\fq^{2m})(1-\fq^{2m}\mu^{2})(1-\fq^{2m}\mu^{-2})\bigr]^{-1},\\
  \kappa_0=V\,\frac{\sigma_0}{1-\mu^2},\qquad
  \kappa_s^{\rm sym}=V\,\frac{\sigma_s-\mu^2\bar\sigma_s}{1-\mu^2},\qquad
  \kappa_s^{\rm anti}=V\,\frac{\sigma_s-\bar\sigma_s}{\mu-\mu^{-1}}\qquad(1\le s\le n),
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
so that $\kappa_0$ is the vacuum character and the $\kappa_s$ come from the
discrete modules.  Then $\Tr1=\kappa_0$ in all three cases; $[A_1,D_3]$ has
$\Tr T=\fq^{-1}(\kappa_0-\kappa_1^{\rm sym}-\chi_1\kappa_1^{\rm anti})$ and
$\Tr D=-\fq^{-1}\kappa_1^{\rm anti}$; and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-a1dodd-seeds}
\begin{aligned}
  [A_1,D_5]:\qquad \Tr s_0&=\kappa_0+\fq^{-2}\bigl(\kappa_1^{\rm sym}-\kappa_2^{\rm sym}-\chi_1\kappa_2^{\rm anti}\bigr),\\
  \Tr s_1&=\fq^{-1}\bigl(\kappa_0-\kappa_2^{\rm sym}-\chi_1\kappa_2^{\rm anti}\bigr),\\
  \Tr s_2&=-\fq^{-1}\bigl(\kappa_1^{\rm anti}+\kappa_2^{\rm anti}\bigr),\qquad
  \Tr s_3=-\fq^{-2}\kappa_1^{\rm anti};\\[4pt]
  [A_1,D_7]:\qquad \Tr s_0&=\fq^{-1}\bigl(\kappa_0-\kappa_3^{\rm sym}-\chi_1\kappa_3^{\rm anti}\bigr)
     +\fq^{-3}\bigl(\kappa_1^{\rm sym}-\kappa_2^{\rm sym}-\chi_1\kappa_2^{\rm anti}\bigr),\\
  \Tr s_1&=\kappa_0+\fq^{-2}\bigl(\kappa_1^{\rm sym}-\kappa_3^{\rm sym}-\chi_1\kappa_3^{\rm anti}\bigr),\\
  \Tr s_2&=\fq^{-1}\bigl(\kappa_0-\kappa_3^{\rm sym}-\chi_1\kappa_3^{\rm anti}\bigr),\\
  \Tr s_3&=-\fq^{-1}\bigl(\kappa_1^{\rm anti}+\kappa_2^{\rm anti}+\kappa_3^{\rm anti}\bigr),\\
  \Tr s_4&=-\fq^{-2}\bigl(\kappa_1^{\rm anti}+\kappa_2^{\rm anti}\bigr),\qquad
  \Tr s_5=-\fq^{-3}\kappa_2^{\rm anti}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Every one is exact to any order.  The two vacua agree at low order:
$\Tr1=1+\chi_2\fq^2+(\chi_0+\chi_2+\chi_4)\fq^4+(\chi_0+3\chi_2+\chi_4+\chi_6)\fq^6
+(3\chi_0+4\chi_2+4\chi_4+\chi_6+\chi_8)\fq^8+\dots$ for both, with $\chi_n$
the character of dimension $n+1$ as in the paper.

\subsubsection{$K_\fq([A_1,E_6])$: an exported presentation, and traces with no frozen data}\label{comp:e6}
The exceptional entries have no hand-written presentation: their cone tables
are generated from the BPS chart and exported, which is a presentation by
generators and known relations.  For $[A_1,E_6]$ the cluster
complex of the $E_6$ quiver is finite: $42$ cluster variables (the
generators, stored as lattice rays), $833$ clusters (the maximal cones, all
of size $6$), and $\rho$ acting on the generators as a permutation of order
$14$ --- the Coxeter number $12$ plus two, as Table~\pref{tab:finite_type}
lists.  The flavour is trivial.  The canonical basis is the set of cone
monomials, and the products of generators that do not $\fq$-commute are the
exported table of $\fq$-Laurent combinations of cone monomials.

\paragraph{The trace.}  $\rho^2$ has order $7$ on the generators, so there are
six $\rho^2$-orbits and cyclicity reduces every trace to seven elementary
traces: $\Tr1$ and one seed per orbit.  Neither needs frozen data or a BPS
chart.  $\Tr1$ is the exact Nahm sum on the BPS spectrum the class carries
--- the vacuum face of the Schur pairing, a finite sum over the charge
lattice at each order.  The seeds then follow from $\Tr1$ by the
orthonormality bootstrap: every canonical $L\ne1$ has $\Tr L=O(\fq)$, the
reduction writes the deep single-generator labels as
$\Tr L=\sum_sP_s(\fq)\Tr s$ over the seeds, and every coefficient of
$\fq^{\le0}$ is one exact linear equation for them.  The values:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-e6-traces}
\begin{gathered}
  \Tr1=1+\fq^4+2\fq^6+3\fq^8+3\fq^{10}+6\fq^{12}+7\fq^{14}+11\fq^{16}+\dots,\\
  \Tr s_0=-\fq-2\fq^5-\fq^7+\dots,\qquad
  \Tr s_2=-\fq-\fq^5-\fq^7+\dots,
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
with the six seeds $s_0,s_2,s_4,s_5,s_6,s_7$ in the generator numbering of
the class.  Computed this way from scratch, the seeds reproduced through
$\fq^{12}$ the table the BPS engine had once frozen, before that table was
removed; through $\fq^{16}$ the computation takes $144$\,s.

Every one of these traces is a finite $\bZ[\fq^{\pm1}]$-combination of the four
characters of the $\mathcal W_3(3,7)$ minimal model.  Write $\chi_h$ for the
character of conformal weight $h\in\{0,-\tfrac37,-\tfrac47,-\tfrac57\}$ as a series
in $q=\fq^2$, divided by its leading power (so $\chi_h=1+O(\fq^2)$).  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-e6-w3}
\begin{gathered}
  \Tr1=\chi_0,\qquad
  \Tr s_2=\Tr s_6=\fq^{-1}\bigl(\chi_0-2\chi_{-3/7}+\chi_{-4/7}\bigr),\qquad
  \Tr s_5=\Tr s_7=\fq^{-2}\bigl(\chi_{-4/7}-\chi_{-3/7}\bigr),\\
  \Tr s_4=\fq^{-1}\chi_0+\fq^{-3}\bigl(\chi_{-4/7}-\chi_{-5/7}\bigr),\qquad
  \Tr s_0=\fq^{-1}\bigl(\chi_0-2\chi_{-3/7}+2\chi_{-4/7}-\chi_{-5/7}\bigr)+\fq\,\chi_0 .
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}

kept as the witness.  The bootstrap's equations pin every seed uniquely through
$\fq^{37}$--$\fq^{40}$, and \eqref{eq:comp-e6-w3} reproduces that solution, so
in that window the combinations are forced by the axioms.  Beyond it they are
the prediction.

\subsubsection{$K_\fq([A_1,E_7])$ with its $U(1)$ flavour}\label{comp:e7}
The presentation is again the cone table exported from the BPS chart.  The exchange matrix $B$ of the $E_7$ quiver has rank
$6$; its kernel, spanned by $e_0-e_2+e_6$ in the node basis of the class, is the
flavour, so the coefficient ring is $R(U(1))=\bZ[\mu^{\pm1}]$ and the flavour
sits in the coefficients.  The table has $90$ generators, stored as rays of the
charge lattice $\bZ^7$, and $3300$ cones: the $2600$ maximal ones, all of size
$6$ and exactly the maximal sets of pairwise $\fq$-commuting generators, and
$700$ of their faces.  The canonical basis is the set of cone monomials.  The
$5880$ ordered pairs of generators that do not $\fq$-commute have their
products in the table, as combinations of cone
monomials with coefficients in $\bZ[\fq^{\pm1},\mu^{\pm1}]$ (powers of $\mu$
from $-2$ to $2$).  $\rho$ permutes the generators in nine orbits of length
$10$, so it has order $10$, as Table~\pref{tab:finite_type} lists with the
flavour $U(1)$.  On elements $\rho$ is twisted by the flavour:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-e7-rho}
  \rho(c\,L_w)=\star(c)\,\mu^{\delta(w)}L_{\rho(w)},\qquad
  \star(\mu)=\mu^{-1},\qquad \delta(w)=\sum_ip_i\delta_i ,
\end{equation}
for the cone monomial $w$ with powers $p_i$ of the generators $i$, where
$\delta_i\in\{0,-1,-2\}$ is the class's table \code{E7_RHO_DELTA} ($41$ of the
$90$ entries nonzero).  The powers of $\mu$ cancel around every orbit, so
$\rho^{10}$ is the identity on elements as well as on labels.  The contract's
label-level \code{rho} is the bare permutation and \code{rho_element} is
\eqref{eq:comp-e7-rho}; orthonormality holds in the element form
$\Tr(\rho(L_a)L_b)=\delta_{a,b}+O(\fq)$, while the label-form pairing
\code{inner_product} is off by a unit character: its $\fq^0$ term at $a=b$ is
$\mu^{-\delta(a)}$ (the encoding split recorded in the source repository's audit record).

\paragraph{The trace.}  Cyclicity reduces every trace to $\Tr1$ and the
traces of the $90$ generators, the seeds $s_i$ (the seed $s_i$ being the
generator $i$).  Unlike $[A_1,E_6]$, the reduction keeps one seed per
generator: on this tier $\rho^2$ on elements differs from the label-level
$\rho^2$ by a power of $\mu$, so the seeds along a $\rho^2$-orbit differ by
powers of $\mu$ rather than being equal.  The $\rho$-equivariance of the
trace, $\Tr\circ\rho=\star\circ\Tr$ (F5 of \S\ref{sec:flavoured}), relates the
seeds along a $\rho$-orbit,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-e7-rule}
  \Tr s_{\rho(i)}(\mu)=\mu^{-\delta_i}\,\Tr s_i(\mu^{-1}),
\end{equation}
so one seed per orbit determines the other nine.  The vacuum is the product
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-e7-vacuum}
  \Tr1=\prod_{n\ge1}\frac{(1-\fq^{10n})^2(1-\fq^{10n-2})(1-\fq^{10n+2})
      \prod_{\pm}(1+\mu^{\pm1}\fq^{10n-1})(1+\mu^{\pm1}\fq^{10n+1})}
    {(1-\fq^{2n})(1-\fq^{2n+2})\prod_{\pm}(1+\mu^{\pm1}\fq^{2n+1})},
\end{equation}
the vacuum character of the Bershadsky--Polyakov algebra at level $-12/5$ (a
literature prior, used here as a measured identity).  With
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-e7-blocks}
\begin{gathered}
  \theta(\mu)=\sum_{n\in\bZ}\fq^{n^2}\mu^n,\qquad
  \psi_a=\sum_{n\in\bZ}\fq^{5n^2+(2a-4)n}\mu^n\quad(0\le a\le4),\\
  \mathrm{th}_r=\prod_{m\ge0}(1-\fq^{10m+r})(1-\fq^{10m+10-r}),\qquad
  \Theta_{ab}=\mathrm{th}_{ab}\,\psi_a\psi_b\quad(0\le a<b\le4),\\
  \mathrm{th}_{ab}=\mathrm{th}_2\ \text{for}\ |a-b|\in\{1,4\},\qquad
  \mathrm{th}_{ab}=\mathrm{th}_4\ \text{for}\ |a-b|\in\{2,3\},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
every seed is a quotient
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-e7-seed}
  \Tr s_i=\frac{N_i}{(\fq^2;\fq^2)_\infty\,\theta(\mu)} ,
\end{equation}
and the numerators of the nine $\rho$-orbit representatives
$0,1,2,3,5,7,12,13,21$ are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-e7-recipes}
\begin{aligned}
  N_0=N_1&=\fq^{-3}\mu^{-1}(\Theta_{12}-\Theta_{13})+\fq^{-2}(\Theta_{34}-\Theta_{24})\\
  &\quad+\fq^{-1}\mu^{-1}(\Theta_{01}+\Theta_{04}+\Theta_{12}-\Theta_{02}-\Theta_{03})+\Theta_{34},\\
  N_2&=\fq^{-2}\mu^{-1}(\Theta_{12}+\Theta_{23}-\Theta_{03}-\Theta_{13})+\fq^{-1}\Theta_{34}+\mu^{-1}\Theta_{04},\\
  N_3&=\fq^{-2}(2\Theta_{12}+2\Theta_{23}-\Theta_{03}-2\Theta_{13}-\Theta_{14})
    +\fq^{-1}(\mu^{-1}\Theta_{01}+\mu\Theta_{34})\\
  &\quad+3\Theta_{04}+2\Theta_{12}+2\Theta_{23}-\Theta_{02}-\Theta_{03}-\Theta_{13}-\Theta_{14}-\Theta_{24}\\
  &\quad+\fq(\mu^{-1}\Theta_{01}+\mu\Theta_{34})
    +\fq^{2}(2\Theta_{04}+\Theta_{12}+\Theta_{23}-\Theta_{03}-\Theta_{13}-\Theta_{14})+\fq^{4}\Theta_{04},\\
  N_5&=\fq^{-1}(\Theta_{04}+\Theta_{12}+\Theta_{23}-\Theta_{03}-\Theta_{13}-\Theta_{14})+\fq\,\Theta_{04},\\
  N_7&=\fq^{-1}(\Theta_{04}+2\Theta_{12}+2\Theta_{23}-\Theta_{02}-\Theta_{03}-\Theta_{13}-\Theta_{14}-\Theta_{24})\\
  &\quad+\mu^{-1}\Theta_{01}+\mu\Theta_{34}
    +\fq(2\Theta_{04}+\Theta_{12}+\Theta_{23}-\Theta_{03}-\Theta_{13}-\Theta_{14})+\fq^{3}\Theta_{04},\\
  N_{12}&=\fq^{-2}(\Theta_{23}-\Theta_{24})+\fq^{-1}\mu^{-1}\Theta_{01},\\
  N_{13}&=\fq^{-1}(\Theta_{23}+\Theta_{34}-\Theta_{14}-\Theta_{24})+\mu^{-1}\Theta_{01},\\
  N_{21}&=\fq^{-1}\mu^{-1}(\Theta_{01}+2\Theta_{12}-\Theta_{02}-\Theta_{03}-\Theta_{13})+2\Theta_{34}-\Theta_{24}\\
  &\quad+\fq\mu^{-1}(\Theta_{01}+\Theta_{04}+\Theta_{12}-\Theta_{02}-\Theta_{03})+\fq^{2}\Theta_{34}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Seeds $0$ and $1$ lie on different $\rho$-orbits and have equal traces.  The
negative powers of $\fq$ in the numerators cancel against the denominator, and
every seed is $O(\fq)$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp-e7-heads}
\begin{gathered}
  \Tr1=1+\fq^2-(\mu+\mu^{-1})\fq^3+3\fq^4-2(\mu+\mu^{-1})\fq^5+(\mu^2+6+\mu^{-2})\fq^6+\dots,\\
  \Tr s_5=-\fq-\fq^3+(\mu+\mu^{-1})\fq^4-3\fq^5+\dots,\\
  \Tr s_0=\Tr s_1=-\mu^{-1}\fq^3-\mu^{-1}\fq^7+\mu^{-2}\fq^8+\dots .
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}

The recipes \eqref{eq:comp-e7-recipes} are measured, not derived.  They were
found by decomposing the seeds by $U(1)$ charge and selected on the values of
the orthonormality bootstrap over $R(U(1))$ through $\fq^{11}$--$\fq^{14}$
(\code{u1_bootstrap.generate_u1}); the orders $\fq^{15}$--$\fq^{20}$ were held out.  Re-implemented
from their text, they give all $90$ seeds equal to the bootstrap's through
$\fq^{20}$, and through $\fq^{22}$ when the bootstrap
takes \eqref{eq:comp-e7-vacuum} as its $\Tr1$ (the orders $\fq^{21}$--$\fq^{22}$
were never seen by the fit).  The bootstrap rests on one recorded hypothesis,
measured and not derived: the coefficient of $\fq^k$ in a seed has $\mu$-degree
at most $4k$; every closed form obeys it through $\fq^{60}$.  Run over the
$\rho^2$-orbits of generators instead of the $\rho$-orbits, so without
\eqref{eq:comp-e7-rule}, the bootstrap gives the same $90$ seeds through
$\fq^6$.  The product \eqref{eq:comp-e7-vacuum} equals the exact Nahm sum on the
BPS spectrum through $\fq^{20}$ and the independent RG flow \code{E7RGKAlgebra}
through $\fq^{14}$; with $10$ replaced by $8$ or $14$ (the boundary level
$-3+3/u$ at $u=4$ or $7$ in place of $u=5$) it disagrees with the Nahm sum from
$\fq^6$ and $\fq^8$.  That the ten products $\Theta_{ab}$ are characters of
Bershadsky--Polyakov modules at level
$-12/5$ is a literature hypothesis, confirmed in the repository only for the
vacuum; nothing the class serves depends on it.

\paragraph{How far the traces are defined.}  Through the cyclicity reduction
a label's trace is a combination of $\Tr1$ and the seeds with coefficients in
$\bZ[\fq^{\pm1},\mu^{\pm1}]$, and $\Tr1$ and the seeds are exact to any order.
That the reduction ends on every label is observed on every label it has been
run on, not proved (the module docstring of \file{src/cone/cone_kalgebra.py} calls its
universality empirical).  The reduction is where the cost lies, and its cost
grows with the depth of the label.  The class runs it on canonical labels
(\code{layer1_on_labels} in \file{src/cone/cone_data.py}): the same cyclicity
identities, with every intermediate a canonical label, every product expanded
by the class's own \code{multiply}, and every label folded along its
$\rho^2$-orbit by $\Tr L_w=\mu^{\delta(\rho(w))-\delta(w)}\,\Tr L_{\rho^2(w)}$,
which $\rho$ above and $\Tr\circ\rho^2=\Tr$ give
(\file{notes/finite_type_trace_machinery.md},
section O).  Labels of the depth the tests use trace in under a second (the
label \code{((21,2),(47,2))}, the cone monomial in the squares of two
$\fq$-commuting generators, at $\fq^8$).  The label \code{((21,2),(47,2),(62,2))},
one more square, traces in $11$\,s and $0.25$\,GB at $\fq^{12}$, where its trace
is $\mu^{-2}\fq^{12}+O(\fq^{13})$.  The reduction on words, which the class ran

At $\fq^{10}$, \code{((6,16),)} takes $194$\,s and $0.9$\,GB, and three labels

principle and computable in practice to degree about $16$.

\subsection{Conjecture~\pref{conj:abeKalgebra} as an algorithm}
The paper conjectures that three conditions on the bubbling corrections
--- Weyl covariance with bar invariance, $O(\fq)$, and the residue
cancellation rule \peqref{eq:star} --- define $K_\fq[G,T^*N]$ uniquely.
The repository turns the three conditions into a solver and the
conjecture into a construction that either returns the unique element or
fails honestly.
\begin{machinery}
implement & \code{AbeKAlgebra} (\file{src/abe/abe_kalgebra.py}): a realisation supplies \code{torus_shape}, \code{chart(label)} and \code{decompose(x)}; \code{multiply}, \code{rho}, \code{trace}, \code{inner_product} are derived.  The acceptance test of a chart is \code{certify_canonical}: bar invariance of every residual (the paper's A1, \code{well_formed_w1}) and a single leading Weyl orbit of multiplicity one with everything else $O(\fq)$ (A2).  The residue cancellation of A3 is \code{star_bubbling.criterion} with \code{wall_partner} for the partner $b=s_\alpha(a)-l\alpha^\vee$: it asks that each pair sum be regular on its wall.  A3's other clause, that every $d_a$ has simple poles only, is imposed by no solver; it is \MEASURED{} on every window line, pure and with matter.  With matter the code imposes the rule on the dressed image $F=Z\cdot f$ through its own trivialization $Z$; the stored lines also satisfy the paper's A3 with the printed matter factors of \peqref{eq:cocha}, and the weights of $N^\vee$ in their place fail it on complex matter.  The equivalent reading in the paper's next subsection --- a well-defined action of the rational torus on the characters $\chi_e(v)$ --- is the repository's own statement of the rule (\file{notes/residue_cancellation_recognition.md}), and is checked on every character of a window. \\

The classes \code{PureGAbeKAlgebra(datum, lines=...)} (\file{src/gn/pure_g_abe_kalgebra.py}) and \code{GNAbeKAlgebra(datum, matter, nf=..., lines=...)} (\file{src/gn/gn_abe_kalgebra.py}) build every label $L_{m,e}$ this way, $m=0$ included, so that the Wilson lines follow from the same axioms; \code{route(label)} reports how a label was built, and charts are memoised and persistable (\code{save_cache}, \code{load_cache} with full axiom re-verification on load). test & \file{tests/test_pure_g_abe_kalgebra.py} (agreement with the fully native \code{PureSU2KAlgebra} at $SU(2)$; the axiom battery, orthonormality and \code{certify_canonical} at $SU(3)$, $Sp(4)$, $Spin(5)$, $G_2$ and non-simply-connected forms), \file{tests/test_g_matter_abe_kalgebra.py}, \file{tests/test_matter_multislot.py}, \file{tests/test_gn_abe_quiver_chains.py}, \file{tests/test_abe_rho_label.py} (the closed form of $\rho$ against the torus twist); K5 on this tier is tested in the source repository. \\

The paper's note that the subdominant support ``appears to follow'' from the axioms is \MEASURED{}: the bubbling lies in $\mathrm{conv}(W\cdot m)$ on every window (\code{tropical_support}), and a bubbling residual grafted outside it, symmetrised over a Weyl orbit so that bar invariance, $O(\fq)$ and Weyl covariance all survive, is rejected by the residue rule alone; its suggestion that bar invariance with $O(\fq)$ bubbling might suffice is the direction the repository took when it retired every constructive route in favour of this single one (2026-08-25); the residue rule is what licenses solving at all on this tier, where an unguarded solve can return an element outside the canonical span that orthonormality cannot detect. \\

\paragraph{What a spec-less entry means.}  It is \MEASURED{}, in three ways.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item A search to depth $12$ rules out only sequences of length at most
    $12$, at every rank.  The closure from weight $13$ found $156$ specs at
    rank $\le 7$ on quivers where the depth-$12$ search had been
    exhaustive.  $154$ of them are longer than $12$ mutations, up to $21$ at
    rank $7$.  The first weight-$13$ round found $136$ more at rank $7$, on
    quivers where that search had also been exhaustive, of lengths $13$

  \item The deep search reaches the misses at rank $\ge 8$: 78\% at weight
    $12$ and 61\% at weight $13$.  It reaches almost none at low rank: $0$ of
    $976$ at weight $\le 10$, and $3$ of $250$ sampled at weight $11$.  So
    long sequences at low rank exist, and they are reached by propagation
    from higher weight, not by search.
  \item $2{,}041$ stored quivers contain an oriented Markov triple as a full
    subquiver.  None of them has a spec.  By Muller's restriction theorem they
    have no maximal green sequence.  The search skips them rather than
    certifying the absence, since the axiom route admits specs that are not
    green.
\end{itemize}
So a \code{crystalline_only@D} entry records that no spec was found, with
the depth and strategy of the search in the manifest.  It never records that
none exists.

every stored spec is also a \emph{maximal green} sequence, with every mutated
vertex green, is \OPEN{}.  That is what the hypothesis of
Conjecture~\pref{conj:cluster} and Muller's theorem speak about.  Sage's
cluster seeds, which only the desktop has, could settle it on the whole
tier.

\section{The battery of tests for the paper's claims: the framework}\label{sec:battery}
The blocks above say, structure by structure, what the repository implements,
generates and tests.  The battery turns the third verb into a programme: an
exhaustive, machine-checked list of the paper's claims and conjectures, each
with a test whose population, controls, evidence class and standing are
stated, and whose results feed back into this document.  It was designed on
21 September 2026, and the registry, the runner, its checks and their
result records are in place under \file{battery/}; the appendix is
generated from them.

\subsection{The registry}
One record per claim, keyed on the paper's own \LaTeX{} labels so that the
handles survive renumbering: the claim's location in the paper, its
statement in the paper's notation, its \emph{kind} (definition,
implementation, theorem, identity, conjecture, coverage, not-testable), the
\emph{population} the
test ranges over, the \emph{procedure} naming the repository functions, the
\emph{controls} (a known-true instance that must pass, a known-false
perturbation that must fail), the \emph{evidence class} (enforced by the
construction, or emergent), what \emph{implements} it today, its
\emph{standing} in the four words of \S1.2 plus \PLANNED{}, its \emph{cost
tier}, and the \emph{environments} it can run in with the inputs each one
needs.  Coverage of the paper is itself a checked property:
the renderer that produces the appendix below refuses to run unless every
label of the stored draft version is attached to a claim or declared
untestable with a reason.  On its first run it caught one omitted label.

A definition is not a claim.  Checking the axioms of Definition~\pref{def:kq}
on a realisation tests that the realisation is a $K_\fq$-algebra, not the
definition; on most tiers an axiom follows analytically from the others or
from the construction, and on the bootstrapped tier the orthonormality
axiom \emph{defines} the trace.  So the battery does not sweep the axioms
over the realisations (the author's ruling; that sweep stays in the
regression suite).  A definition's row records instead, tier by tier, where
each axiom is enforced by construction and where it is emergent, and points
to the rows that test it there: the paper's statements that a construction
\emph{yields} a $K_\fq$-algebra --- the pentagon, $U_\fq(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$,
$[A_1,D_3]$, the Coulomb-branch conjecture, the skein algebras, the flow
constructions --- on which the verifiers run only for the axioms emergent on
that tier.  Consistency of a definition is witnessed once by its first
model, which the paper supplies (the quantum torus for
Definition~\pref{def:kq}); a formal consistency or independence statement
would need a proof assistant and is out of scope.

The first checks, in the author's order, are therefore not the axioms
over the realisations but: (a)~does the \code{KAlgebra} contract express
the axioms of Definition~\pref{def:kq} correctly --- a transcription of
each axiom beside its verifier's formula, for the author to review, and
an executable half, five toy realisations each violating one axiom and
each rejected by that axiom's verifier; (b)~the quantum torus is a
$K_\fq$-algebra analytically, so the check is that
\code{QuantumTorusKAlg} implements Example~\pref{ex:qt} verbatim; (c)~the
pentagon, ``where the first meat is'': that the stand-alone class
implements Definition~\pref{def:penta} (relations, basis, $\rho$), that it
is a $K_\fq$-algebra with all five axioms emergent, and that every printed
formula of the section holds.  Rows of this shape carry the kind
\emph{implementation} (the author's ``implements correctly'').  All eight rows were run on 21 September 2026 in the web
environment: 79 individual checks, none failing; the appendix prints each
run beside its standing, and the measured numbers --- the discrimination
matrix, the orders at which the recursion's $A_n, B_n$ approach the traces
($2n+1$ and $2n+2$), the order to which $-A_n/B_n$ determines the ratio
($n^2+n-1$, sharper than the $n^2$ the paper states), the convergents'
orders $(d+1)(d+2)$ --- are in the result records.  A Lean formalisation
of the pentagon example is being attempted on the author's local machine;
the pentagon rows' checks are the specification it has to meet, and a row
proved that way will name its Lean artefact beside its standing (the
author's ruling: a field, not a new standing word).

\subsection{Two execution axes: cost and environment}
A claim's execution is described by two independent fields.  The
\emph{cost tier} says how long a run takes: \emph{fast} --- minutes, the
existing suites and thin adapters over the existing verifiers, run with
every change; \emph{extensive} --- hours, the same checks with windows and
depths that grow from run to run and are recorded with the result, and
every sweep over a BPS-quiver dictionary.  The \emph{environment} says
where the run can happen, in the author's own two words for the two
machines this project uses: \emph{web} --- the tracked repository is the
whole input, so the claim runs in the web session and in continuous
integration; \emph{local} --- the run needs data that only the author's
local machine holds, the gitignored dictionary builds (the enumerated
build beyond the shipped weight, $E\ge 13$, the flavoured staging builds,
the older weak dictionary with its 579{,}293
entries and 1{,}115{,}973 mutation edges).  The distinction is a
distinction of \emph{inputs}, not of machines: a web-sized run made on
the local machine is still a web run, so populations stay comparable,
and the registry names for each environment the files a run reads.
The Seiberg--Witten side lives on both: the conjectures on spectrum
generators (Conjectures~\pref{conj:S-unique}, \pref{conj:pbw},
\pref{conj:cluster}, \pref{conj:upper}, \pref{conj:mut}, \pref{conj:factored}), the
acceptance census of \peqref{eq:quiver}, the sign and relation checks on
charts, and the flavoured constraint \peqref{eq:flavouredquiver} each
have a web-sized population --- the shipped tiers, every strongly connected
quiver with total arrow weight at most $E=12$ (every other quiver answered
by composing its components) and the ten flavoured tiers, with the population read from the dictionaries' manifests rather
than written into the code --- and a local-sized one, and a tier that
ships moves its quivers from the second column to the first without a
change to the battery.  A run that asks for the local environment where
the local inputs are absent is refused and logged with the missing path;
it never produces a result.

\subsection{Controls, evidence, results}
No test enters the registry without a positive control; a green check is
printed as a regression guard where the property is built into the
construction and as evidence where it is not.  A run of a claim writes one
dated record --- the environment and machine it ran on, the inputs it read
with their manifest fields and digests, the population actually covered
(windows, depths, cells), the pass and fail counts, the controls' outcomes ---
and the appendix is regenerated from the registry and the latest record per
environment, so what this document reports is what was run, and where.  The
local machine's evidence travels as these small committed records while its
data stays off the remote.  The appendix that follows is
that generated table: each claim's standing, and the latest record of each
of its runs.
